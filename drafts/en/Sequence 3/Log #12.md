# Log #12

As the soundproofed door of the Saturn 8 closes behind us, Lou and I enter a dim room that serves as a cloakroom for the customers. Under the muted lights, I note that the hangers are empty. I deduce the bar was still closed when the malfunction occurred. That's already a good sign. The place wasn't stormed by creatures. The other thing that tells me we're somewhere safe is the cleanliness. The elegant tiles covering the floor up to the mid-height of the walls are free of a single drop of blood. It's more like I'm the one ruining the picture with my clothes and the smell of death soaked into me.  
"Over here!" a voice throws at us.  
At the far end of the cloakroom, I recognize the one who let us in. Half his body sticks out of the doorway of a swing door opening onto the main space. Behind a porthole, I think I make out other people.  
"Come up to here and don't move!"  
His finger points at an imaginary line two meters from us. Behind him, I hear a few voices whispering to him not to let us through under any circumstances, but he shuts them up. I take the chance to quickly check his social media profile. The guy's name is Daniel. He's in his thirties and he's a bartender at the Saturn 8. He must fancy himself the master of the place a bit, with his little burgundy tight-collared shirt and his silver earring. It gives him a pretty-boy look, but ever since we got here he hasn't been very pleasant. Maybe it's the situation, or maybe this guy's simply a big jerk. I don't know, but his disapproval of me doesn't leave his mug. In other circumstances, I wouldn't have given a damn — I surely couldn't have gotten in anyway, and Lou would have been turned away because of her age — but right now, all that's stirring in my mind is finding out whether Fara came through here, washing off all my filth and, if possible, sleeping for twenty-four hours. So I step forward. Lou stays behind me. For now, she's followed the instructions, but I can feel her boiling at my back and, just as we take a single step, Daniel raises his hand at us to forbid us from going any further.  
"Damn it, wait a sec," I said. "You're not at home here!"  
I stop. He's wearing me out, but I try to stay courteous.  
"Man, why did you let us in?"  
Daniel goes red with anger like a baboon's butt. Even his bald skull takes on color. Looks like he's having a hard time handling the pressure the whispers at his back put on him. I clench my fist. Their muttering does my head in. I keep Lou close to me. I won't hold out long. I step in.  
"Damn! Maybe we can all talk together, no?"  
Daniel still has his arm raised as a precaution.  
"Shut up," he answers me curtly before turning back to the others.  
Next time he talks to me like that, I break his teeth. I press.  
"If you opened up for us, it's because you know Fara, right?"  
My exasperation seeps into every word and all at once, the swing door opens to let three other people through: a woman, two men. Along with Daniel, they surround us while keeping a certain distance. At the same time, it's not too surprising, I stink something terrible. The nausea reads on their faces. This is starting to seriously wear me out. Me, I just want to collapse in a corner and grab a nap. I deserve a nice little warrior's rest, the family-size kind, and everything that goes with it, no? In the end, I'm starting to wonder if we're safe. The four clowns putting sticks in our wheels look like they want to throw down, but I doubt their ability to make my life hell; they don't have a scratch on them. It's as if the apocalypse spared them. Maybe it's just because of their AR skin. I don't know. I doubt it. They wouldn't be in this state if they'd met the same violence that fell on us. What also strikes me, from their clothing styles, is that they're clearly not part of the Saturn 8's staff. They're odds and ends of every age and origin. And from their faces, they're scared out of their minds to see someone show up who's been through hell and bears the marks of it. It must remind them how big a bunch of weaklings they are.  
"So it's this little turd, then?" one of the guys spits.  
His name's *E. Chauvet* and drops of contempt fall from his mouth.  
"That's the name the lieutenant gave us, anyway, Daniel affirms."  
I can't believe it. Instead of talking to me, they have their own quiet little chat.  
"It's Fara you're calling the lieutenant? I flare up."  
Their faces scowl the moment I open mine.  
"Yeah, that's right!" the girl standing near Lou throws out.  
The label *A. Rocheleau* floats above her head. The third guy stays silent, but he looks like he's enjoying the show. Honestly, they all look like a band of scared, privileged scumbags. I'm even downright disappointed the update didn't do a bit more weeding among them; they wear me out so much.  
"So what are you waiting for to call her over?"  
Daniel rolls his eyes as if I'd said something really dumb.  
"What do you think? She'd surely have welcomed you if she were here."  
A snide smile draws itself across their lips. Lou pings me.

***Lou***  
> These guys are actually morons? (ー.ー゛)  
> I know a few kung-fu holds that really hurt, if you want.

With a wave of my hand, I calm her. *E. Chauvet* takes the chance to open his mouth.  
"She left to save other lost causes like you."  
A big gob of spit lands on my chest. Lou's hand squeezes my arm.

***Aymeric***  
> If I clock one of them, I'll let you handle whoever you want.  
> For now, we've gotta go easy.

Daniel speaks up again, but I can feel he's not doing it with much goodwill.  
"Put yourself in our shoes, man. We're just wondering why the lieutenant asked us to watch for the arrival of a filthy guy like you. There are already a lot of us here, and maybe it's chaos outside, but I don't want the Saturn 8 to become a *nons* hole, okay? If we agreed in the first place, it's because you were supposed to show up with gear. What did you do with it? Sell it to pay for some junk? Me, what I see is a shady guy who reeks so bad he doesn't deserve to come in here."  
My muscles tense. I hold back and lift my head.  
"I'm exhausted. I don't feel like fighting, but disrespect me one more time and I'll rip your nose off with my teeth."  
My voice is calm and steady. Daniel shudders and takes a step back.  
"Tsss," *E. Chauvet* throws out with disdain.  
That's the affront too many; Lou explodes.  
"Damn it, are you going to cut the crap?"  
Everyone goes wide-eyed. Lou continues.  
"You're like rabid dogs sniffing each other's asses. Aymeric saved my life more than once, you know! So your troll comments, you can keep them. I imagine you've seen the square's full of corpses, that people killed each other, and that you've lost every member of your family?"  
Lou's voice goes out for a second before picking back up with force.  
"And you're wary of him because he smells bad? I don't even know what's worse in a moment like this: your kindergarten-grade nonsense or that it's a fifteen-year-old girl giving you a moral lesson."  
Nobody utters a word anymore. Lou's got character. I had no doubt about it.  
"What's going on here?"  
Her anger has drawn a fifth accomplice, who pokes his head through the opening. His name's *J. Leroy* and he looks rather put out to discover everyone's gathered in the cloakroom without him being invited.  
"Get lost, Julian!" Daniel suddenly tosses at him.  
A middle finger goes with the words, but the so-called Julian couldn't care less. In fact, seeing us, Lou and me, he gets his smile back and pushes the door open with his shoulder without hesitation. His hands are loaded with two cups and a thermos. Nobody invited him, but at least he had the elegance to bring drinks. It's just missing some little soy petits fours. In any case, the result is that his buddies pull long faces. It's just that they'd organized everything to have a good time and he comes putting an end to their welcome party, like when cops show up in the middle of a wedding to demand you turn the bass down a notch.  
"I'm not interrupting, I hope? Julian even dares."  
Then, as if nothing were wrong, he sets his gear down on the cloakroom counter without minding the sighs born at his back. I really like this guy. He naturally commands respect with his hands and his stevedore's build. I'm not surprised he gets at least a minimum of respect when he walks into a room. A real dad, you know! The most surprising thing is that despite his rugby-player frame, he gives off a certain tenderness. It surely comes from his laughing eyes, which seem to know the value of life. I don't know. There's something inexplicable about it. From his profile, he's in his forties, with a husband, three kids, and a dog named Rudolf. I become aware it'd be better to speak of his loved ones in the past tense. Lou's right: *you've lost every member of your family*. We're going to have to get used to it, but I can't help thinking about them. Despite our disagreements, I hope they're okay. I don't really believe it, but a goddamn doubt lingers all the same. In a way, Lou can draw a line under all that; she watched her father die before her eyes. Me, I wonder if I'll see them again one day in some form or another. So, when I close Julian's profile, my heart tightens in my chest.  
He had the perfect family.  
"Don't mind them," he tells us, opening his thermos.  
His eyelids crinkle with mischief.  
"We're going to start the introductions over from the beginning."  
The others sigh as he points at them casually.  
"So here's Daniel, Agathe, Éric, and…"  
Julian stops on the fourth, who hadn't spoken up yet.  
"Chahid, right? He's mute, I think."  
The one concerned, leaning nonchalantly against a wall, finally opens his mouth.  
"That's not true," he grumbles. I'm just watching.  
Julian smiles at him like a father at his son's first steps.  
"Sorry, Chahid. Everyone can make a mistake!"  
At his back, Daniel fumes. Looks like he can't stand being made a fool of by a big bruiser with a tender heart. I get it, but it's so satisfying that the tension that was twisting my guts is now stretching my smile muscles. Lou looks like she's finally relaxing too. She follows with her eyes as Daniel refuses to drop it. He steps up to Julian and grabs his arm just as he was about to pour the steaming liquid from the thermos into a cup. The steam tickles my nostrils. We're facing a good hot freeze-dried coffee just the way I like them. My neurons cry out from withdrawal as Julian resists Daniel's pathetic grip without trouble. It's a riot watching him play tough guy.  
"Go back to dealing with the others," Daniel articulates, jaw clenched.  
"That's exactly what I'm doing, kiddo. I'm dealing with the others."  
I feel like I'm watching an arm-wrestling match where the stakes are how this whole microsociety is going to treat us. We already know who's going to win, by the way; there's no contest between a puppet who thinks he's the boss and a brick wall whose bicep is so imposing that my two hands wouldn't be enough to wrap around it.  
"Good thing I heard the kid screaming from the hallway," Julian goes on, because I seem to recall I told you to let me know when Aymeric arrived. Fara's going to come back at some point, and if there's one thing you don't want, Daniel, it's for her to kick you out of here with a boot to the ass.  
He then turns to the three others.  
"And same goes for you," you hear! Let me do my job...  
The little group lowers their heads like children caught red-handed. Agathe grimaces. Éric and Chahid drag their feet. All three are the first to leave the cloakroom under Julian's stern gaze. Daniel holds out a second. I even think he swallows back all the spit he was about to hurl in Julian's face, then he disappears in turn. Behind him, the swing door closes, whipping the air. Calm returns. Julian has a way with the troublemakers. Looks like he's handled this kind of pettiness his whole life: a real pro. All at once, I feel like I'm someone privileged — I didn't dream it, did he say my name? — and, given the surprise it stirs on my mug, he must notice it, since he sets his big mitt on my shoulder. It gives off a warmth that pours like a shiver through my body. Lou joins us. She can't stand staying there saying nothing anymore.  
"Thanks," she says before introducing herself.  
The cheeks of her AR skin blush exaggeratedly.  
"Pleased to meet you, kiddo! Julian enthuses."  
Then he shakes her hand with his bear's grip. Lou lets herself be jostled.  
"Glad you made it here safe and sound," he points out, pinching his nose.  
"The smell's that terrible, I worry."  
"Once you've had a good shower, we'll all be able to breathe."  
Did he really say the word *shower*? I'm in seventh heaven right now.  
"You've got those here, showers?"  
Julian's face lights up with kindness.  
"Yeah, they're in the dressing rooms. You'll see, it feels amazing..."  
He then hands us a cup each, along with a wink.  
"First, drink this. Then, I'll take you there."  
An awkward smile plasters itself on my face. I thank him and knock back the coffee.  
"And after that, you'll have free rein to eat and rest."  
As Lou looks at what's hiding in her cup, she grimaces.  
"You wouldn't have something else for me?"  
"Sorry, it's all I had on hand."  
Lou's shoulders drop, then she ends up swallowing it all reluctantly.  
"For special orders," Julian specifies, you'll have to see Daniel. Him and the three others had taken over the place before Fara showed up. She kind of forced them to take people in by invoking some decree or other and threatening them with a little paralysis. She can't sit still. When she brought me here with other survivors, two hours ago, she left again right away, but Daniel still acts like the Saturn 8 belongs to him. In any case, don't worry, he won't hurt anyone as long as I'm still alive. He's too scared of me.  
A hearty laugh escapes his open throat, then he gathers his things.  
"Come on, let's go! Follow me! We'll be better off inside."
