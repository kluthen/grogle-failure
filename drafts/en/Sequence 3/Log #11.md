# Log #11

In the morning mist, I finally make out the gigantic branches of the Liberty Tree rising above the buildings. We're almost there. Commemoration Square is one step away — the Saturn 8 two — but I'm so wiped that if Lou weren't pulling me by the arm, I'd be wandering at random. This kid impresses me. She's got one hell of a nerve to have managed to drag me like an old bum all the way here. Give me back my quilt and my houseplant! On top of that, I look like nothing. Since the massacre earlier, I've got holes in my clothes, I'm covered in blood, and my left arm is all sore. On the other hand, I can't complain about my bones; they've healed completely. On that note, Gus congratulated me by handing me a badge to celebrate the end of my recovery: *Stay Peachy, kiddo*. I even got virtual pink balloons that floated up before fading into the air. I have to say this personal assistant has a way with rewards, but I find it pretty weird. Then again, the designers surely never imagined we'd live through this kind of event. PumpThatBody got in on it too. He and Gus insist I buy a higher-performance bracelet. As if I had nothing better to do than go shopping. And when I refused, they accused me of doping. So I muted them both. This ball-busting they're doing doesn't help me grasp what could have happened for me to be able to smash all those faces.  
What am I, really? A big brute who needs to drink in violence? A gallant medieval-style knight rescuing princesses? I have no idea. And deep down, it's not that bad not to know. All that matters for now is that Lou is safe and sound. In a way, she gave me a reason to keep going, and I owe her one. I'd surely have let myself die in that armor without this rich little pain to motivate me. And truth is, she didn't just motivate me. When she came down from the rooftops to find me, she helped me up. It wasn't a sure thing. Even though it reeked of guts, me, I wanted to enjoy my victory, to stare at the sky, watch the clouds gather, and do the starfish in the middle of the corpses. Then a drizzle started wetting my face. I cut the interface, no more notifs, total bliss! And Lou was getting impatient. I remember her calling me, but I didn't feel like hearing her. After a while, she decided that *dipping her shoes in the puddles of entrails* wasn't as terrible as it looked. In the moment, I'd have preferred she keep her distance, then I got a good slap to reconnect to reality. It snaps your head around, stings a bit, but it works like a charm. I was on my feet in a few seconds, ready to set off for the Saturn 8.  
Before taking off, Lou let me in on something useful. She's got a sharp head on her, the kid, because while I was fighting like a devil to save our lives, she figured out I liked things that hit hard. Got to say, that fence bar that fell into my hands was like a blessing. The trouble is, once the job was done and my hatred drained off, I could barely lift it. So Lou brought me back a hammer that was lying around in a storeroom under the apartments. It put a smile back on my face and eased the sting of her hand on my cheek. With that, I was set — almost good as new — but exhausted all the same.  
So we stayed careful. All the more since the city's gone very quiet. Each of our steps gave me the feeling we could be spotted from meters around. So we preferred to dodge the main thoroughfares and crossed through the buildings that seemed deserted, forcing the doors. We zigzagged a bit, have to admit it, and didn't gain all that much time, but it beats coming face to face with a horde. Even so, we had to defend ourselves as best we could against the few solitary hysterics that crossed our path. In those rare cases, Lou figured she could *pull* the less alert ones. That's a *gamer* thing, *pulling*. She must have learned it playing online games. The principle's simple — a bit cowardly, really. Lou just acts as bait, drawing the critters' attention one by one to me. With a few finger snaps, or a pebble, and a video share of her view, I manage to take them by surprise when they're about to jump her. I aim straight for the skull and it's settled in no time with my super hammer. For this, Lou was very strong; she knew how to hold back her screams. It looks easy like that, but even if you defend yourself to survive, killing always does something to you. It's like a big mix of relief and guilt, then horror and pity. I don't really know how to analyze it and I don't know if I'll manage to get used to it. In any case, as for my teammate, she soothes her conscience by covering her eyes. Then again, on the road, there was one time we really scared ourselves coming across a hermit crab, but I think it was sleeping snug in its shell. I'm not even sure it saw us.  
"We're getting close," Lou suddenly whispers to me.  
I tense up before shooting her the darkest of looks.

She surely said that to make conversation or to unwind, but right now I mostly need to focus.  
We've got one last move to play.  
We have to cross a pedestrian street that opens directly onto the huge Commemoration Square, then slip into an alley. Up to now, this square, we've worked to skirt around it. When we arrived from the south, we got a bad feeling, the kind that makes the hairs on the back of your neck stand up. It reeked of death and, in the dull morning light, the mist camouflages everything. We had no way to see the buildings on the other side or even a piece of the Saturn 8. Now we're so close that I can make out big shadows, the size of two men, navigating through the fog. The nauseating smell has gotten sharper too. We were right not to go that way, since the cobblestones are strewn with corpses outnumbering all those of the charnel houses we've come across; I plug my nose.  
All around us, many shop windows have been broken open and still display the government messages anyway. Lou isn't reassured. She presses against me. I think she's understood we're getting a bit closer to hell. At the same time, Commemoration Square is a commercial zone and, when the world stopped turning, it was seven in the evening. After leaving work, naturally, people gathered en masse to prepare their evening, do some shopping, or grab a drink. It must have been carnage. In the end, I'm satisfied I started the adventure tucked away warm in a little prison cell. No doubt about it, it was vile to be stuck and witness those atrocities, but it surely wasn't as much as here. Here, it looks like the whole population rushed toward the Liberty Tree to wage their last battle, scattering the weakest among them along the way. I have a hard time imagining it, but I shudder, grasping the scale of the disaster and some of its aftermath. Near a sidewalk, the body of a child lying face down tears at my heart. Further on, a woman is slumped in a shop window. She couldn't get rid of the shard of glass piercing her chest. An old man curled up like a fetus against a wall. Between his blood-covered clothes, I glimpse his face, even paler than the sky. I shiver. Lou begs me to move on, but I still have to check a couple of things.  
Caution, caution!

What unsettles me, though, is that everything's quiet around us, if you ignore the presence of torn-off limbs and corpses heaped in the corners. Even on the local communication lounge, it looks like the messages have calmed down. I see no videos or photos, no absurd conversations. There are a few lines looping like Lou's father did, but nothing else. I check my radar. There too, no significant movement. Fara's green dot doesn't show up nearby. That pisses me off big-time. I hope she waited for us, but with all the critters we crossed, who knows what she might have run into. Lou notices it too. She must have amused herself going through the list of the 323 connected.

Lou accepts the deal. I feel like I'm talking to her like a five-year-old, but if it lets her accept the constraints, I'll take it. I grab her hand. It's trembling. This time, I'm the one who's going to lead her to the objective. She's done enough. With a tilt of my head, I signal that we're about to set off. It's the right moment; the shadows that were navigating the mist have vanished and the street's still deserted. We crouch and move forward. We pass the abandoned child, the impaled woman, and the ashen old man. We come across other bodies too. I can't figure out their age or gender. I only see them pass in my periphery. I don't want to dwell on them. I try to flush all this out the way you'd scroll past the profiles that bore you on social media. They're just images, photos, nightmarish representations from a tortured mind. I set them aside. I block my thoughts. If all goes well, tomorrow I'll have forgotten their faces. I tell myself a story. I imagine this arm I'm stepping over is nothing but a garbage bag lying on the public way, or that these prints running along the sidewalk are just the tracks of some unlucky soul who stepped in a giant pile of crap. It borders on the ridiculous. You defend yourself however you can. Lou doesn't leave my side. Her hand squeezes mine every time she shudders. I hope she kept her eyes closed. I wish it for her, and I lead her safe and sound all the way into the alley. We're here. We made it!  
My lungs fill with oxygen. I realize I made the crossing holding my breath. I hide. Lou flattens herself with me against the storefront of a shop spared by the apocalypse. A few windows now separate us from one of the Saturn 8's entrances. We stop thinking, we hug the walls, we pass the still-intact display windows of a ready-to-project skin boutique. Behind the glass, the virtual mannequins look us dead in the eye as if to invite us in. This kind of advertising trick always freaked me out, but it doesn't give me the slightest shiver anymore. In a few hours, we've dealt with much worse.  
Suddenly, Lou stops. Her face relaxes, then she points at the fluorescent sign of the Saturn 8, standing out against walls covered in government messages. The club's door sits just below, set into a recess. We quickly slip into it. We're sheltered from prying eyes and hysterics. I sigh with relief. We wouldn't have gone any further anyway; the Saturn 8 is on the corner between our alley and another big artery that opens directly onto Commemoration Square. The smell filling my nostrils wrings a heave out of me; we've never been so close to the fog and the shadows peopling it. The club itself takes up the whole ground floor of the building. On the first there's an extension of the fashion store we just passed and, above that, over several floors, are apartments.  
In this district, the buildings have nothing to do with the austere housing printed for the masses, even if they're still made of concrete. The architects worked the reliefs for a most realistic effect. Visually, if the building's AR projections still held in place, you'd see an ashlar-stone skin appear that you'd mistake for the real thing. Anyway, all these particulars no longer matter. And me, I despair; on my radar, Fara's green dot stays absent. I expected it, but that doesn't stop me from raging in my corner. I wonder how long I can hide this from Lou. I hold out a bit longer. Facing me, the girl takes the initiative to grab the latch. Without a sound, she tries to open the door, which stays shut. Despairing, Lou then lets herself slide against the wall and ends up sitting on the ground. I meet her crumbling expression.

We pushed so far into the city — we're so tired — and I'm the one who dragged her all the way here. Across from us, on the other side of the alley, a luxury hotel rises like a castle in the middle of the mist. There are several of them around the square. Before the end of the world, I couldn't have spent the night in any of them, except by sacrificing a month's salary or playing maintenance man. I start dreaming of finding us a room there, sinking into a bathtub the size of my apartment, and rolling around in clean sheets. I'm completely raving, but still: if the hallways aren't full of monsters, we could sneak inside. And they have kitchens. I'm sure their fridges are loaded with real ice cream, real fruit, and real cuts of meat. We'd stuff ourselves, we'd rest, and the next day, we'd flee to reach a hamlet forgotten by everyone outside the city. Now that's a great idea, isn't it? No?  
Yeah! It could have been, but a flash of light interrupts my fantasies. Stuck to the front door, like a giant peephole, an AR window appears. It stretches, blinks, then relays to us the face of a bald man in his thirties. His skin looks so smooth and his forehead so shiny that, for a moment, I wonder if he isn't a virtual bouncer, the kind of AI that analyzes your outfit and your social media profile before telling you you're not welcome. But I quickly realize I'm mistaken; his rather annoyed expression and the AR label floating above his head confirm he's indeed a survivor and that his name is *D. Albuin*.
