# Log #15

Before joining the team, I find Lou in the main hall. She's playing on the stage with her new friend and the otter *Pocket Beast* he never parts with. The animal, lying on its back, spits rings of water like others blow smoke rings. They float, grow. When they're the right size, Lou leaps through them, arms forward, like through a hoop, and lands on the other side with a roll. She's wearing yet another new outfit: leggings worthy of the greatest gymnasts — turquoise, knotted at the waist with a wide red ribbon — and, of course, her inseparable cat accessories. I'm starting to believe she's got a whole wardrobe in her bracelet. In any case, the boy applauds her. I watch them. After a third jump, Lou botches it and ends up on her butt. The two of them laugh. You'd think the world still spun more or less round for some. My heart pinches a little. They're having fun. I hope they too make it to the bunker and that our commando mission won't be a fiasco.  
As they keep up their stunts, I approach. I avoid the pit. I pass near the bar. Fara's talking with Daniel. They pay me no attention. Further off, other survivors keep eating their meals. Some even take an interest in the show the two kids are putting on. When I set a foot on the stage, I feel the looks at my back. I get the feeling I'm interrupting something important, but I couldn't care less.  
"So, what did Fara want with you?" Lou throws at me, seeing me show up.  
"Grown-up business!" I let slip to make her fume.  
I laugh a little. Lou punches me in the stomach. I mime the guy suffering like hell and I jump her to muss up her head. She bursts out laughing as she struggles. It feels good to mess around all the same. After thoroughly mussing up my little ward, I notice the boy watching us with a mortified look. Lou gets serious again then and turns to him.  
"See, Yannick, he's not mean!"  
The named one grimaces before coming to hide behind Lou as if hiding in his mother's skirts. His *Pocket Beast* runs in pursuit and jumps onto his shoulder. I almost get the impression the creature has a life of its own, but I know full well that virtual companions are designed to react to their owners' commands. The animations, for their part, always keep a good share of randomness to make the thing more authentic. So I crouch and hold out my hand. I try to show I'm more interested in his otter than in him, but the animal takes an attack stance. It narrows its eyes, bares its teeth, and spits a virtual jet of water in my face. For a second, the AR images it sends me pass through me and a bluish veil covers my field of vision. I then wave my hands in the air as if to clean myself, I hear a few laughs in the room, and the kid's as he hides all the more.  
Lou's laughing too.  
"Nice one!" I throw at Yannick, smiling.  
His otter threatens me again as if it had me in its sights.  
"If you come closer, I'll do it again!" the boy tells me.  
I straighten up, raising my hands high.  
"I'll leave you alone, little guy. Promise!"  
Lou mocks me, then she invites Yannick to play in his corner.  
"So, she starts again, what was that private discussion about?"  
She drags it out of me with tweezers, it seems.  
"Are you jealous, actually?"  
"Pfff, not even!"  
Faced with her sulky mug and her cat ears, I cave.  
"We're just going to take a trip outside to grab a bit of gear."  
Lou pales slightly, then pulls herself together.  
"When do we leave?"  
"Uh, let me stop you right there, squirt. You're not coming on the trip!"  
She scowls.  
"I already told you I'm not a kid anymore."  
I roll my eyes. Lou adds:  
"And besides, I know how to fight. Look!"  
Like a spinning top, she throws herself at me. My eyes go wide, then I feel a draft on my face. I jump. Her foot just grazed my nose.  
"They don't scare me, those things," she adds, setting it back on the ground.  
I sigh. Despite her fire, I feel obliged to calm her down a bit.  
"It should scare you, though! That's how we'll survive, by being careful and by being scared. If we start acting dumb and charging into the fray, we'll all get eaten before reaching the bunker. So just: *stay calm*!"  
Lou huffs as if my little speech had no coherence.  
"Yeah, right! You're the first one to charge into the fray!"  
I clear my throat, then I grumble before deflecting the conversation.  
"That's not the point!"  
"Oh yeah?" Lou throws at me, trying to come back to it.  
I resist.  
"Fara told me you had a combat trainer that'd be useful to me!"  
Lou tilts her head to the side.  
"Yeah, we talked about it with her and Julian earlier. Why?"  
"Can you tell me about it a bit?"  
As she ponders a way to explain the principle to me, I think I see little gears turning above her head. I blink. They've vanished. I don't know if it's my imagination at work or an AR projection, but Lou uses so many emoticons you end up getting lost.  
"So," she begins, it's the trainer I use to play CRO.  
"What's this crap acronym now?"  
"Catacomb Raider Online, ugh!"  
"The thing where you play treasure hunters in ruins?"  
"Yeah, it's just so so good!"  
Her eyes start shining, then she picks back up.  
"It's RealPhysics compatible."  
My mouth twists, then I launch into a few guesses.  
"It's the same as the official vSport engine, is that it?"  
"Yeah, basically. You've never heard of it or what?"  
"Of course I have, just that it's not really my thing."  
Lou nods. She's understanding.  
"It means all the martial arts the instructor teaches can be practiced in the real world by doing real movements. It's not movements like: I move two fingers and it casts a healing spell, no! It's more like, I do a pirouette and throw a kick. The avatar also does the same pirouette and throws the same kick. So it's super handy for my competitions and demonstrations like I already showed you — or even against real people! I don't know if you follow?"  
"Yeah, I follow that very well…"  
"So then?"  
"Why would Fara have pointed me toward you if it's the same thing?"  
Lou knits her brow. I think I'm coming off like an old man.  
"There are tons of realistic martial arts in the CRO version, but also martial arts made to defend against animals — bears, tigers, wolves — or others made to kill monsters of all kinds. Me, for example, I know how to fight against creatures that use their tail to sweep your legs, and I know how to manage against a thing with three arms. In CRO, I learn to spot the signs of this kind of attack and react accordingly. You'd see, it's really comprehensive!"  
Her speech gives me shivers. I feel like I've crossed into another world and I'm pulling a dumbfounded face. I'm starting to think Lou would indeed manage better than me against this type of monster.  
I retort:  
"So that's why Fara finds it so interesting?"  
Lou nods in affirmation.  
"I imagine so, yeah! And it's weird to think it's going to be useful for real…"  
"But how's it going to adapt, anyway? There are super different monsters."  
"I don't really know, I'm no expert, you know..."  
The little gears above her head reappear.  
"Real-time video analysis, maybe?"  
"Maybe…"  
I rack my brain to try to grasp the thing, but Lou interrupts me.  
"So, you want the package?"  
I nod.  
"Well then, here goes!"

***L. Humbert shares the executable file:*** CRO Training System v 6.13.2 CE

But before I even accept, Lou throws at me, looking embarrassed:  
"Uh, by the way…"  
"What?"  
She grimaces.  
"You're going to need a better bracelet."  
I clench my teeth.  
"The one from the Town Hall," she adds, it doesn't support these apps...  
The world's really conspiring against me.  
"You never changed it, actually?"  
I feel my muscles tense.  
"No! What did you think? Not all of us have a rich dad..."  
Lou swallows. Her eyes fix on the floor. I should probably have kept my mouth shut.  
"Sorry," I breathe, rubbing my head.  
Then I immediately get back to our starting subject.  
"Fara also told me Julian could help me?"  
The trouble that occupied Lou's gaze suddenly vanishes.  
"Oh, yeah! I saw on his profile that he was a CRO player."  
At least there'll be someone more mature to guide me through RealPhysics.  
"Anyway, thanks. I won't be long, then…"  
Lou squirms. I get the feeling she doesn't much want me to leave.  
"Take care of yourself," I tell her, "and keep your eyes open!"  
The kid nods in affirmation, then I add before splitting.  
"I don't really trust that bastard Daniel and his band of morons."  
As I leave the stage, I launch the package installation. Of course, I don't have net access. So the app chews me out a bit, but it lets me access the AR trainer anyway. A window offers to customize him. I plant myself in a corner, against a pillar. There are tons of possibilities: guy, girl, or a mix of both; human or not; the voice, the look, the clothes. There are too many parameters. I don't bother. I randomize the thing. Characters are projected at full size in front of me several times until I land on a trainer I like well. I rename him Jaz. I make him more supple than powerful. He's rather tall like me and, like me, he's mixed-race. He's got pointed piercings on his brow, a ring in his nose, a fluorescent-yellow mohawk, and a tongue split in two like a snake's. He greets me when I confirm the creation, then immediately gets annoyed by the version of my bracelet. I sigh and let out loud:  
"Eat shit, you goddamn piece of crap bracelet!"  
Two people at a neighboring table eye me sideways. With a certain irritation, I cut the app. I grumble. The clock's ticking. I'll deal with this problem later. I then remember Fara asked us to read the information she gathered on the monsters. So, before rejoining my expedition companions, I open her file. It's actually pretty concise.  
Fara classified the creatures that stand on two legs as *homines*. A simple, effective name, I think. I scroll through the photos showing them, you could say, at different stages of evolution. There are first the young, freshly converted, who aren't physically very different from human beings. In fact, that's the bulk of the monsters I've come across on my way. They're just hysterical, aggressive, and not very intelligent. They go after the first thing that moves. They charge into the fray without thinking and it usually ends badly for them. The ones who win out tend to become little *Mikes*, like the one who knocked me out in front of Lou's place. Fara writes: *individualists who toughen up, grow, gain muscle and bone mass*.  
I switch to the next sheet: the *canids*.  
Apparently, Fara renamed them that because they stand on all fours and look a bit like a dog. That seems perfectly justified to me. A close-up on a corpse shows me, by the way, the joints that transformed to let them run faster and their teeth-filled maws that widened. Even so, their faces stay very close to a human's. It's very unsettling. Fara writes: *unlike homines, canids hunt in packs and don't attack each other. Group mind. Easy to beat if the individual is isolated, but to be fled at all costs in the presence of several of them*.  
I grimace before moving on: the *constructs*.  
In the photos, I recognize shapes familiar to me. Lou's father was a construct, and my buddy the hermit crab too. What Fara calls this, in fact, are humans who fused with cars, capsules, drones, or any other originally lifeless thing. Apparently, according to her, they seem to hide inside objects and use them as a shell. The problem is, they manage to stay camouflaged until the last moment, and you have to be careful. But there's a trick: AR identification can help us avoid them. Basically, if I see a trash can named Jean-Michel Parmentier, I don't go near it. I crack up all alone, then I tackle the last type Fara was willing to catalog: *the amalgams*.  
Those, I've never come across. And given Fara's shots, I consider myself a lucky guy. The images she handed us come, for the most part, from the cameras pointed at Commemoration Square. They show us these creatures from several angles, wandering in the morning mist around the Liberty Tree. I then understand these are the shadows I glimpsed with Lou arriving at the Saturn 8. It literally twists my guts, because what I see is really gross of the grossest. I feel like I'm in front of one of those historical documentaries, the ones where they tell us about the old wars and the genocides, the ones that prove to us humanity isn't so human after all. By the way, I immediately understand why Fara decided to call these creatures *amalgams*. My throat tightens. I look away, but the image stays printed on my retina.  
These things, these amalgams, are heaps of corpses agglutinated to one another, living and moving charnel houses that move at a snail's pace but are half the size of the square's buildings. In fact, it's a bit as if some sicko Nazi had decided to put a hundred or so people in a big mold and press very hard. The result's chaotic, but it holds together in one piece. It's a misshapen mass of bloody skin, torn tissue, and AR projection, a mass of faces expressing pain never seen before, a mass of outstretched arms and legs flailing at random. It looks like Hell in the flesh. Something straight out of a medieval Christian painting. All it's missing is little red devils with their pitchforks and the apocalypse becomes reality.  
I sway and catch myself on the pillar behind me. With a blink, I close the window, then I check Fara's notes. They're very short: *stay away at all costs*. It doesn't reassure me, and it reassures me even less to know these things are a few meters from the Saturn 8, just a few meters.  
I come to my senses when Julian passes by me.  
"You okay, little guy?" he throws at me, stopping at my level. "I was looking for you!"  
I swallow.  
"I just finished reading the briefing…"  
"Oh!"  
His face splits into a grimace.  
"It's chilling. I'll give you that!"  
"Especially the amalgams. Have you come across any?"  
"Briefly! And luckily, they're not very reactive. On the other hand, what makes them especially dangerous is their weight. They can so easily roll over you if you're not careful. I wouldn't want to get caught by one of those horrors. It's a sure way to become like them, you see? Then, got to say, just looking at them, you could wet your pants. I promise. I've seen nasty stuff — I'm a nursing assistant originally — but this: we're at the peak of gore!"  
With a sigh, he sets his big mitt on my shoulder.  
"All that's left is to hope the outing goes well, eh!"  
Then he hands me a printed sandwich, ham and butter.  
"I'd promised you something to perk you up."  
I stare with undisguised disgust at the artificial cold cuts, squeezed between his two slices of flat sandwich bread, and I wave the whole thing away with the back of my hand.  
"I'm not really hungry anymore! But thanks..."  
Julian blinks a bit.  
"Your loss!"  
Then he downs the whole snack.  
"Follow me," he then tells me, mouth full. "I thought of something!"  
A piece of bread lands on my shoes. I ignore it.  
"Okay, okay!" I breathe, complying.  
I leave the room with him. On the way, I think back to the briefing images: Fara didn't mention even once any snakes or other critters I fought during my fit of raging madness, at the foot of Lou's building. She saw my videos, though. I quickly send her a message to complete her bestiary. I tell myself my stay outside probably taught me more than her little file. It forges your character to find yourself in the middle of a crowd of monsters, for real. I even come to hope my comrades lived through the same thing. Otherwise, they're going to get their money's worth…  
"You see these things?" Julian asks me.  
We've gone back to the entrance hall, near the cloakroom. He points at the queue stanchions stowed behind the counter, then grabs two of them and pulls them over to the other side. I understand where he's going with this and I help him unscrew the bars from their bases. Julian manages first. He hefts his makeshift weapon as if holding an axe and spins it with his wrist. You'd think he was a goddamn Viking about to set off to battle. Even if Julian's not wearing animal skins or a shield, I can totally picture him with the war paint to match. In fact, you can tell right away he's a RealPhysics user. He's got fluid movements and looks at ease. I'd be surprised if he learned to fight in hospitals.  
"Need a hand? he teases, seeing me struggle."  
I refuse, then I end up pulling the stanchion out of its base myself.  
"So, what do you think?" he throws at me.  
His two eyebrows lift with mischief.  
"I admit. It'll do the job..."  
It's a bit heavy and cumbersome, but it'll be effective enough as long as it doesn't slip out of our fingers too much. Even so, my meager street experience tells me there's a strong chance we'll drop them along the way. I'm a bit sad all the same; I traded my super badass armor, my electric baton, and my gun for an old stanchion grabbed in a rush. I rage in silence and, while I practice reproducing the same wrist movements as Julian, the two other guys who are supposed to come with us make their entrance. Olivia's with them and I want to say: damn, now that's a woman! Because she didn't skimp on the gear: a two-handed sledgehammer, a hard hat, gloves, and big boots. She's even wearing a leather jacket that's most certainly seen better days. In short, she's got her Smasher outfit — Smasheress, even — except it's stained with blood and the handle of her sledgehammer has already taken a few hits. Got to say it's not meant to be used as a weapon: usually, walls don't fight back.  
"That's all you found?" she tosses at us.  
She sizes us up with a certain disdain. It's true we really look like a band of screwups with our gear. But well, apparently, we're the only screwups Fara managed to gather. By the way, the two grumblers from earlier aren't any better off. They're dressed as plainly as me. In fact, you wouldn't think that in a few minutes we're going to set off on a little trek-and-fitness jaunt that risks turning into a nightmare.  
"What are you looking at like that?" she snaps at me.  
"Nothing, nothing!"  
At a push, you could think we'd just hired Olivia for a striptease because one of our buddies fantasizes about construction outfits, but she's not even wearing booty shorts. I feel really dumb. I chase the idea from my mind and look at the two other guys laughing. They keep to their corner and talk a bit as if they'd known each other for a while. They call each other by their first names: Raoul and Crist. Crist for Cristobal, I imagine. The poor guys! Me too, I'd be pissed at society if my parents had chosen to name me that. When they realize Julian and I scrounged up a *quality* weapon, they decide to dismantle the queue stanchions too. While they get ready, Fara arrives in turn. She's geared up in her armor and a pistol she's already holding in hand. She doesn't even speak to us, but sends us a message straight to the group chat.
