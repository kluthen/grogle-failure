# Log #14

I leap up like a devil as a voice pulls me from my sleep.  
"Easy, everything's fine!" I hear in the dimness.  
My heart races. Sweat runs down my forehead. I feel like I'm surfacing from breath-holding that lasted centuries. This woman's voice rings a bell. I know it. It's Fara. She's back and her massive shadow stands right beside me.  
"Sorry, I apologize. You scared the crap out of me..."  
A spark of joy chases off my surprise as I try to adjust my sight to the darkness, then all the baggage tied to my memories comes back to me like a slap in the face: the end of the world, the monsters, the dead, and the weird things that fell on us at every street corner. My guts twist at once and I worry about Lou. Fara has set her heavy hand on my shoulder. I feel the cold contact of her armor. I'm surprised.  
"Did you get back a while ago?" I ask her like a friend.  
I can't see her face, but I feel her fingers tense.  
"We'll chat later," she lays down.  
Then she repeats that everything's fine, stressing each word. It doesn't give me a very good impression about the reason for her presence here. On the contrary, it sounds like everything is not fine. My guts agree with me; they keep twisting like wrung-out rags. I try to stand, to face her, but Fara forces me to keep my ass on the carpet.  
"I'd need your presence," she adds.  
Just as I'm about to ask why, she continues:  
"We're going to plan our relocation. We can't stay in the Saturn 8 forever. My objective is still to reach the bunker. So, get dressed properly and join us in the management office. Understood?"  
Fara, true to herself, is already giving me orders like a private.  
"I'll be there in a minute," I let slip, sighing.  
"Very good!" she commends me. I left a coffee on the table.  
Two AR invitations offer me to join the group conversations named *CivilianSurvivors_1* and *ConscriptSurvivors_1*. I accept without quibbling. From the names, I have no doubt Fara's behind all this. It gets a smile out of me like a kitten's teary eyes, then I pull on my t-shirt.  
All in all, I only slept four hours. It's almost one in the afternoon, my stomach's growling, and I'm astonishingly surprised to be this fresh after such a short nap. I slept like a log, but I still walk on autopilot to the bathroom to splash some water on my head and chase off my dark circles. Already better! Back to it: I gulp my coffee in one go and I head out. Perfect! It's the kick I needed. It's almost a normal day shaping up, like I'm leaving for work, except today I'm not going to stress out answering morons who can't connect their sneakers. My depressing little life got atomized by the update. We may complain about them, but we get too easily addicted to these cursed notifications, and their absence hands me a good dose of extra anxiety.
