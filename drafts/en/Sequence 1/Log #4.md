# Log #4

A spasm drags me back to reality.  
I hurt like never before and the pain twists my guts like a fork in a plate of spaghetti. I don't even have the strength to open my eyes anymore, and despite the agony tearing through me, an immense weariness washes over me. I'm floating. My thoughts chain together without my being able to grasp them. They fly over me. My father looks at me and walks away. My ex tells me she's had enough. Everyone thinks I'm dead. Nobody cares about me anymore. It's a hell I think has no end. I'm sinking, but my surroundings catch me. Just a few steps away, I hear a breath dying out. It's not my breath. In here, it reeks of hemoglobin, but I also catch other, far more nauseating smells. I'd rather not imagine where they come from. I don't know where I am. I've lost all sense of time. The walls are shaking. An explosion compresses my heart and an adrenaline surge struggles to wake me.  
Behind my closed eyelids, Gus strives to throw me a bit of info.

> Nanomachine calibration... Complete.  
> Recovering physiological data.  
> Alert: Internet connection severed.  
> The authorities have activated Protocol 3.4.87 - State of emergency.  
> Altercation: attempt to alert the police.  
> Unable to reach the police: local incident report issued.  
> Injuries detected.  
> Launching the Diagnostic application.

***Diagnostic***

- 9 fractured bones  
- 26 contusions

> Life-threatening condition.  
> Unable to reach a medical assistance system.  
> Unable to reach an ambulance.  
> Please proceed to the nearest hospital immediately.  
> Launching the Survival protocol.  
> 12 minutes before irreversible degradation of the vascular and cerebral systems.

Three windows join Gus's; I no longer know which to focus on.

***Public Announcement - Bayeux II***  
> Alert: State of emergency declared.  
> Please return to your home and do not leave under any circumstances.  
> Await further instructions.

***Local Communication Lounge***  
> 23 people connected within a 50m radius.  
> 67 unread messages.

***PumpThatBody (1 notification pending)***  
> Wow! Now that's my champion!  
> **. . .**

The intimate darkness behind my eyelids gets overrun by interfaces working themselves into a frenzy. Everything is red and blinking to coax me out of my lethargy, but my mind can't answer the prompts. Slowly, my eyelids finally let in a dim light. I see blurry, but I make out the red and white of the emergency alerts plastered over the walls. The loudspeakers have stopped broadcasting. A distant explosion makes the walls vibrate. I have to get out of here. I want to get up; my arms don't respond, my neck stays locked. I think I've reached a high-ceilinged room where all the neon lights are off. Only the glowing banners of the announcements are left to dress the surroundings in a monochrome veil. I'm apparently sitting, propped against a wall like an old doll someone left in a corner. To my left, there's someone. I don't know if he's still human, but he's still alive. His chest swells with air, then sinks back weakly. A trickle of black blood runs from his mouth. He'll probably die in the coming minutes. Like me, actually, if I do nothing.  
Facing us, I recognize what looks like a counter. For some reason I don't know, I'm hidden behind it, sheltered. What got into me? I don't know. How did I get here? No idea. The good news is, I left my cell and all those guys who wanted to tear out my insides are gone. Frankly, who cares. Sure, Gus could give me some information about what happened to me, but he was too busy calibrating my nanomachines. Even so, he's proud to inform me that I've only got about ten minutes left to live. I don't feel the slightest fear. Why should I worry? I'm going to go out without suffering — technology's a beautiful thing. I think about my houseplant, which is going to die all alone. Really? There's no one else who matters? What a crap life!  
For the occasion, I want to see one familiar mug again, PumpThatBody's. Pathetic? Probably. Especially since he's just going to tell me to do pull-ups, to work on my endurance or my cardio.  
In the end, if I'd had a girlfriend, that would've solved the problem.

So, if I'm understanding what PumpThatBody is telling me, I'm not actually dying and I've just got a bad cramp? What a joke. He must not see many athletes in this situation, or else he's trolling me so I'll go in peace, with the feeling of having accomplished something extraordinary. Wouldn't even surprise me. But come on, damn it! These numbers make no sense. Even back when I still took my exercises seriously, I never gained more than 0.15 at a time. I'm not getting any of this — I give up — and since I'm going to die soon anyway…  
I open a chat window.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Local Lounge

I want to shout that I exist one last time, even to strangers, to anyone really. I just hope someone will hear me. Usually people don't use the proximity comms. On those channels, you mostly find an infernal flood of ads and a few unhinged people who haven't grasped that nobody's interested in them.

***Local Communication Lounge***  
> 21 people connected within a 50m radius.  
> 78 unread messages.

A tidal wave of incomprehensible messages floods over me and terrifies me. I'm not the only one who had the idea of connecting here, but I notice that most of what's being said is purely incoherent. Sometimes the sentences make no sense, sometimes the words are illegible. Like someone ran thoughts through a blender before reassembling the letters at random. And then there are the images all these people have posted. They give me a glimpse of just how far gone this world is. There are torn-off limbs, pools of blood, people fleeing and failing to. And sometimes I come across close-up videos of entrails. The frenzied, the zombies, the enraged — or whatever they are — still wear their NeXT bracelets and, like good citizens, they broadcast their misdeeds. My stomach knots. Even me, a connoisseur of nasty stuff on the net, I can't bring myself to take in all this. It's easy to gorge on violence when you live on the other side of the world, but when it comes and craps on you, that's another thing.  
Still, in this vile mass, I decipher a few messages.

> If there are any survivors left, speak up.  
> I'm going to try to get down to reach the armory.  
> I scroll through other horrors before I find a second message.

> I've still got an auto-injector.  
> I'm coming!

***F. Nakach***

This exchange dates from less than a minute ago and the number of connections to the lounge suddenly drops from twenty-one to nineteen. A shiver runs through me. I imagine what might have happened up there. *T. Rickard* is no longer on the list of connected people, but there's still a certain *M. Tesnov* — maybe this famous Mike — and the one who calls himself Lieutenant still seems to be alive. I see a little hope in it. My fogged mind stirs. I don't know who you are, *F. Nakach*, but you can still save me.  
I don't lose a second more. I put out a call:

***A. Bator***  
> Help!  
> I'm a civilian.  
> I don't know where I am, but I've got maybe ten minutes left.  
> If you're still there F. Nakach, think of me!

I count the seconds, watching for an answer on the channel, but all I get is a flurry of disjointed messages. Someone keeps repeating that they're really very hungry. It makes me uneasy. Another posts a string of letters with no rhyme or reason. I strain my ears. A door slams in the room. I hope it's him coming to my rescue. I stay quiet all the same; whatever just came in seems to me to be moving too slowly for a human. It drags its feet. As it passes, it shoves furniture that screeches across the tiles. I grind my teeth. Objects shatter on the floor. I hold my breath, then send one last message on the local lounge.

***A. Bator***  
> F. Nakach, is that you?  
> I think I can hear you.  
> I'm propped against a wall.  
> I can't move.

The footsteps stop; I shudder.

***F. Nakach***  
> That's not me, but I know where you are.  
> I'm coming as fast as I can.  
> Hang on…

My hair stands on end. If it's not my rescuer who just came in, then who is it? I'm scared of finding out before my body gives out. On the local lounge, it's panic. A wave of insults drowns my field of vision. I decide to hide the window and shut my eyelids for good. A mantra spins in my skull, the one belonging to children who hope to vanish under their covers: *I am one with my mattress, I am one with my mattress, I am one with my mattress*.  
In the room, other doors slam, but still no Nakach. This place draws every lost soul, it seems. Suddenly, a scream rings out — I tense — then another answers it. I don't know if these creatures understand each other, but now sounds of splintering wood and breaking glass ring out. Blows are raining down, I think. Conversation's not their thing, and good for me. I just have to stay still. I just have to hold on a bit longer. I try with all my strength, but I feel the nanomachines' effect on my body. I no longer have any reason to worry. I'm so calm, in fact, that I don't even flinch when something heavy and spongy crashes just above my head before falling back onto my knees. I'm so calm that I no longer care about the warm liquid soaking my trousers. My eyelids press a little tighter. The interface windows go dark one by one. Only one is left, my personal assistant's.

***Gus - Critical Alert***  
> 2 minutes before irreversible degradation of the vascular and cerebral systems.

Two detonations still manage to pull me from the void waiting for me. I dare to open my eyes. I fight against a leaden sleep. My sight blurs. A shadow runs toward me. Its outline stands out against the glowing announcements. I feel like a demon's coming to save me. It brings its hand toward my neck. I sink.
