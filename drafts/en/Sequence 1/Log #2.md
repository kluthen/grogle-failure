# Log #2

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Recent activity log.

***Gus***

> Opening of an administrative control channel by Inspector D. Martinot.  
> Revocation of personal rights.  
> Disconnection from social networks.  
> Internet access restricted.  
> Extraction of information relevant to the investigation…  
> .  
> .  
> .  
> Extraction complete.  
> Calendar entry: Case review by Judge R. Einen, tomorrow at 2:15 a.m.  
> Closing of the administrative control channel.

Good thing I can count on Gus to keep me company.  
I sigh. With all this, I could be raging, but no. I could want to smash everything, but no. It's not on me, after all. And yet I stay calm. I'm a bit of a fatalist. Besides, I don't even hold it against these cops. In the end they're no better than us in this whole system. They just follow scripts even more rigid than ours. The only difference, maybe, is that they chose a job where half the time you're busting heads. Okay, I'm caricaturing a bit; the trip's been quiet for me so far. They were kind enough, despite my vision being out of commission, to activate movement assistance so I'd follow them like an automaton. If after that you don't feel like a robot, I don't know what would do it. For Alex, I imagine they broke out the stretcher. That's standard procedure when you take a jolt.  
On the way, I got a nice padded lecture on the laws we're not supposed to be ignorant of:

***Bayeux II Police***  
> The drug known as *Dream* is an application for nanomachines that induces hallucinations similar to those projected by virtual reality capsules. Its danger comes from its ability to overload the nanomachines with information, which can then cause internal burns, brain lesions, and a complete disconnection of the nanomachines from your NeXT bracelet's control network.  
> By preventing the State from monitoring your vital signs, this risk of disconnection constitutes the first offense related to the use of *Dream*, as well as a direct violation of the 2075 law on the non-proliferation of neo-pathogens. This disconnection therefore entails a second offense of obstruction of justice, this time related to the violation of the 2117 law on the control of the NeXT bracelet by law enforcement. Furthermore, the *Dream* application does not comply with the CE and NF standards that guarantee the physical integrity of the user and those around them.  
> Penalties incurred & Maximum sentences: failure to report an offense to the authorities: 2 weeks of public works and 3 days of suspension of citizen's rights.

These two laws are the pillar of our modern world — at least, that's what they drummed into us at school. The first came with the democratization of that bracelet everyone wears today, the NeXT. It was created for our safety, and above all for the safety of those in power. They come in every shape and color, but every citizen, from birth, gets a standard one at the Town Hall that they can swap out every year. It's a bit like a handcuff binding us to the State. It follows us everywhere. Not wearing it means losing your citizenship… and that comes with plenty of consequences. *Your NeXT protects you against cancer and disease*: that's what came out of our teachers' mouths, and what gets published regularly on the State news feeds. They also always tell us that non-citizens are unwitting epidemic hotbeds and that they must therefore be reported to the authorities. These *nons*, as we call them, I've had occasion to cross paths with a few during our outings with *Smasher*, and it's true that when you see their faces, you can only congratulate yourself for having that famous bracelet. Their pockmarked skin, sometimes discolored or even holed in places, reminds me why I prefer to fall in line. Some say the *nons* are no longer human, but not me. I don't wish them any harm. I even feel a bit sorry for them sometimes. They don't scare me. Actually, they flee from us every time as if we were demons; the threat of a police raid keeps them at bay.  
As for the second law, the one from 2117, it's more complicated. For one, it was made very clear to us that the whole country was in chaos getting it passed. Loads of controversy, scheming, and above all a lot of protests and riots. People didn't think it was right that law enforcement could directly control their bodies like puppets, or blind them at will. It's an insane power, a crazy amount of pressure, the ultimate violation of privacy. Honestly, I'd have taken to the streets if I'd lived back then. But me, I was born into all this crap. I know what it's like out there and, anyway, we don't get a say anymore. You live with the pressure or you eat it in the face. A bit like me right now… while an iron grip guides me to my new quarters.  
All of a sudden, they sit me down. I feel the warmth of the metal on my ass; this little corner of paradise must have housed another guest not long ago. I grimace. For now, I still see nothing but a whitish haze on which Gus feeds me a bit of info about my vital signs. Nothing alarming. Then, when the door closes, my vision is returned to me. Relief loosens my shoulders. A heavy load's just been lifted off me, but the smell of sweat and urine, typical of holding cells, fills my nostrils. Space-wise, I'm familiar: two square meters surrounded by thick plexi walls. Just enough to stretch your legs, but not enough to avoid cramps. The last time they put me in here, I was terrified, and it was my dad who got me out. I was a kid. My mother didn't even lecture me. She hadn't given a damn about me for a long time already.  
Apparently our friends the cops dumped Alex in the cell next door. I can see him through the transparent strip that strips us of any sense of privacy. Poor Alex, maybe he's not tetanized anymore, but I figure he must regret playing dumb. I remember that when I went through it, the slightest movement became excruciating; for four days I couldn't get any sleep. Their crap ravages you from the inside like you wouldn't believe. Then again, that's not really what's driving me crazy right now. The thing torturing me is that they've completely cut us off from the networks and the news feeds. I'm left alone with myself. It makes me think about uncool stuff I usually keep well away. And then there are all the appointments we're going to miss without being able to warn anyone. Émeline and Farid are going to lose it if they can't reach us. Tonight's raid, I can kiss that goodbye. And little Olivia, who'd caught my eye, all I can do is hope nobody snaps her up before me. It's driving me nuts!  
I glance around. In the nine other cells, men and women wait their turn. Without the AR to glamorize us, we're run-of-the-mill individuals, no style, no personality. By reflex, I run my hand through my hair. It looks like nothing without a good *skin*. Among my neighbors, some are really dirtier than the others. Makes you wonder if they're not using the benefits of our new technologies to hide the fact that they never wash.  
At the far end, a girl paces back and forth and kicks at her seat. In another cell, a guy acts like he's totally at home. He's stretched out on the floor with his legs up in the air and you can hear him snoring. And then there's Alex's neighbor, who spends his time mocking him. The guy keeps repeating on a loop that Alex is too dumb for not complying, then cackles like a lunatic. It chills my blood to be stuck with types like this. So I end up sitting back down on my deluxe bench that doubles as a toilet bowl. They thought of everything to make our stay pleasant. All this to say that silence is not exactly what defines this place. At least, not until everyone abruptly falls quiet all at once; an AR message has just appeared before my eyes.

***Groogle NeXT***  
> Launching a critical update.  
> Do not under any circumstances power off your bracelet before the download is complete.  
> Estimated time remaining: 1 minute.

My limbs tremble at an announcement like that.  
"What the hell is this!" a first detainee snaps.  
"Am I seeing things?" Alex growls at me from his cell.  
I can't manage to answer him. My throat tightens.  
"Shitty dictatorship," my other neighbor spits.  
The last time the citizens got hit with a critical update was over thirty years ago, during the year of the 2117 riots. The State was enforcing its new law, the one that lets law enforcement blind or tetanize fugitives who haven't grasped that they can't fight it. Half a million broadcasts, interviews, and official announcements framed the whole thing. People were warned of the exact hour that would tip them into this new system. They'd even received directives in case of failure. Of course, I didn't live through that event, but it left its mark all the way down to my generation.

***Groogle NeXT***  
> Estimated time remaining: 20 seconds.

I clench my teeth. I don't know what's waiting for us and it terrifies me.  
As the download advances, five police officers burst out in front of the cells. They've put on reinforced vests and each keeps his baton within reach. I don't understand what they're doing here.  
Alex catches my eye. He looks like he's freaking out too.  
"Son of a bitch," his neighbor screams. "Republic of my balls!"  
One of the guards bangs his baton against the plexi.  
"Stay calm! Or I'm adding insubordination to your file!"  
Another cop mutters off to the side. I think I hear:  
"We don't know any more than you do anyway."  
One second left. The installation kicks off. The progress bar races forward. Everyone — even the cops — falls silent and stares into space, focused on one last message pulsing in our field of vision.

***Groogle NeXT***  
> Restarting bracelet...
