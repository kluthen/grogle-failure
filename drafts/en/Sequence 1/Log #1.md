# Log #1

***Alex to Aymeric (6 messages pending)***  
> Come on Aymeric, it's just *Dream*, man!  
> **. . .**

His notifications blink across my retina; he's getting on my nerves.  
He won't quit today. He's after a real thrill, but at 25 I'm still dragging myself along the bottom rungs of our modern society. I don't feel like adding *junkie* to my résumé. And anyway, come on. I know Alex. He loves a bad idea. In a month he'll want us to flog the stuff if he ends up liking it. And I don't exactly fancy ending my days in prison with a dealer's label stamped on my forehead.  
Cut off from the system, that's no life.  
And at the same time, this life right here — my life — it's a long way from any *Dream*.  
I repeat the same stuff all day long:  
"Good afternoon Ma'am, your indicators are flagging an excessive heart rate."  
"Good afternoon Sir, the shoes you ordered have arrived."  
"Good afternoon, your appointment wasn't honored, would you like to reschedule?"  
A robot could do my job, but they tell us people online need a human touch these days. It's the spirit of the age: hyperconnectivity. By drifting apart from each other with all our machines, we're supposedly desperately short on human warmth and contact. So, *exit* the automated systems and hello human personal assistants. Not that surprising, really. We live in a society where personal services are pretty much all that's left to make a living. If you've got nothing upstairs, that's where you end up working — even if, in the end, all we do is follow a script written by those very robots we're trying to avoid.  
It's ridiculous, but what's the alternative?  
Either way, at least I get a 30-square-meter flat. I don't live alone. I've got a roommate. Nothing original, obviously, since all my neighbors live in the exact same box, and probably all my coworkers at the call center too. But I make do. Even my boss isn't better housed, except he shares his living space with his wife. I don't even know why I'm complaining. There's far worse out there: I've got my own food printer. It delivers *thirty flavors for varied, balanced meals*. I've also got a houseplant, a gift from the Town Hall. It's so unkillable I sometimes wonder if it's not plastic. I've had to water it once in two years. Always the same face. On bad nights, instead of talking to my roommate, I talk to it under my breath.  
Damn, that's grim actually. I really need to find myself a girlfriend...

***Argos Corporation - Session Manager***  
> Please vacate your station.

Six p.m. already. Didn't see the time go by. Then again, scripts save you from thinking. They let you work without really listening, without really being there. You can be thinking about the girl who flashed you her tits the night before while telling some little old lady her husband's in intensive care. Anyway! If I'm told it's time to go, then it's time to go. I kick off the procedure to leave my work pod. It's a big metal shell that gives us access to the General Network with the magic of virtual reality. That way you can work on a sunny beach or on top of Mont Blanc. You forget the gray. You're more productive. Today I was set up in a Japanese temple, lost in the heart of a bamboo forest that dissolves when I unplug the umbilical from my bracelet.  
The capsule opens like a jewelry box and I surface, hauling myself out of the suspension gel. A few drops fall to the floor. The rest drains off the machine to be recycled. I try not to think about it too much. I know that tomorrow I'll have to dunk myself back into the same liquid other coworkers will have used before me. Sounds a bit gross like that, but in the end, tap water works on the same principle. Here, at Work Complex 4#, they assure us there's no risk, but it still disgusts me to know I'm swimming in other people's fluids. You never get used to it. But at least, when you keep your head down for a year like I do, you earn the right to a fifteen-second shower. Living the dream. Way to go, me!  
And just as the water's rinsing the gel off me, Alex pings me again.  
I'm not going to keep him waiting any longer. I cut the video feed — I'm naked — and open the chat window floating in front of me. He's carpet-bombed me.

***Alex***  
> Come on Aymeric, it's just *Dream*, man!  
> You're not answering?  
> Out of my pod. Waiting for you outside the cafeteria.  
> Aym?  
> Maybe Tom fed us a load of crap.  
> Maybe the dealer won't even show.  
> You there? I know you're there! Saw your post on the company group…

I shoot back a quick reply, by thought.

***Aymeric***  
> Dude, take it easy, please…  
> Give me two seconds?

***Alex***  
> I'm at the cafeteria right now! It's gonna be amazing, you'll see.  
> Like a treasure hunt, there's a map, a key to find, secret phrases.  
> And at the end, a *Dream* life — even if it doesn't last as long ;-)

He's wearing me out, but I'm intrigued by how elaborate the whole thing is; I give in.

***Aymeric***  
> Fine, but don't count on me to buy that crap!

He's won. I'm going to go with him. Maybe I can talk him out of it on the way. My dad always told me you've got to stand by your friends, even if that means getting in their way when they're about to do something really stupid. Deep down the old man's not wrong, even if he doesn't have any friends. Besides, I owe Alex one. He's pulled me out of bad spots before, back when I was going off the rails. But lately he's the one with the worst ideas in the crew. This is just one more example. I don't know how far down he's going to sink if this keeps up.

***Alex***  
> Laterz, bro!

His last message wipes from my vision just as the shower cuts out; my time's up. I pull on my clothes. Nothing fancy. The choice of fabric, of colors and styles, that's for people who've got credits to burn on nothing. Us, down at the bottom of the ladder, we treat ourselves differently. We wear standard stuff in cotton recycled an unholy number of times. It's gray, unisex, and it's got one advantage: you can project an augmented-reality skin onto it — AR, as we say.  
With a simple mental command, I pull up a work suit. We're still on the floor that belongs to Argos: might as well stay decent. Outside, I'll dress in something more laid-back, jeans and a bomber. I don't want to look like some little lapdog in a tie working in an office. Same goes for the hair. We go from a near-military crop to the short mohawk I like. Thank you, AR skins! Saves me having to deal with the mop of frizzy hair I inherited from my mother.  
So, off to the elevator, where I have to physically rub shoulders with my coworkers. When I step inside, everyone's staring into space. They look like plastic mannequins. Really, they're just busy checking their messages. During work hours we don't have access to anything but the session manager's feed, a few coworkers', and our own tasks. There's a bit to catch up on, basically. It's all in the service of the *human warmth* we sell at Argos. You've got to give it your all. So I get to catching up too. I quickly check on the news feeds I'm subscribed to.  
Hey! One of the people in the elevator is also signed up to the *Smasher* group, but I've never seen her before. Her name's Olivia. Probably new. Maybe I'll see her tonight for the Raid, once we've sorted out the *Alex* problem. I hesitate about messaging her. I think better of it. My mind's a bit busy with other nonsense, so I add a task to my list. My personal assistant, Gus, will take care of reminding me about all that when the time comes.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Reminder.  
> Look into Olivia.  
> Try a soft approach for flirting.

***Reminder***  
> Successfully added to your reminders.

The elevator finally arrives. Everyone shoves to get out. Once outside, I look up. It's a nice evening in *Bayeux II*. Makes a change from the rain we usually get, and since I hadn't planned on roaming the streets at all, that's pretty good news. I'd happily take the chance to have my one real meal of the week and, along the way, console Alex when his mysterious dealer stands him up.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Finances Status.  
> Enough for a restaurant?

***Finances Status***  
> You have 235 credits left in your current account.  
> I'm forwarding you a list of restaurants compatible with your budget.  
> Warning: I'd advise against dessert.

***Aymeric***  
> Send an invite to Émeline and Farid too.

***Gus***  
> Invitations sent.  
> Weather note: conditions will hold for the evening.

Now, off to meet Alex. He's standing around outside the cafeteria. Big windows give me a glimpse of the people gathered inside. It's happy hour. Everyone's put on their favorite AR outfit. It can hurt your eyes fast, the styles people wear are so eclectic and changeable at the speed of thought. Alex is no exception, with his wrecked-looking mug and his red-checkered trousers. He waves at me. He doesn't even wait for me to reach him before setting off. I follow. We cross the streets at a jog. He knows exactly where to go.  
All around us, Bayeux II rolls past. This new town is just a string of identical copy-pasted buildings, concrete, nothing but concrete, straight lines, wide open, standard gray like our clothes. And like our clothes, the walls get covered in AR décor. It's easy to forget our crap lives with all these colors. The old town of Bayeux is long gone. Only the old folks who live here ever really lived it. The worst moment was when the cathedral collapsed as the waters rose. Must have been wild. Today all that's left are empty houses and squats: a real treat for us.  
On the way, we pass near the commercial arcades. Alex tells me we have to go further, that we have to reach the edge of the Hexagon. Out there you find a few street hawkers selling goods of dubious origin or stuff you only find outside the authorized channels. The police swing by regularly to check they're not breaking the law too badly, and as long as they don't bother anyone, their presence is tolerated. For now, that doesn't scare me. Alex is very chatty. You can tell he's all worked up at the idea of reaching the end of his adventure. It always feels good to talk to someone made of flesh and blood. We cling to this real life, no script to follow. We argue about our tastes in AR skins, about the ever-growing mess of virtual ads hovering over public space. It's nice, you know! And as we're listening to each other, for once talking face to face, an intrusive notification cuts into our exchange. My virtual fitness coach, a stocky, peppy little guy in the cheesiest yellow leggings, pops up in a window, a towel around his neck.

***PumpThatBody***  
> Hey Buddy!  
> Don't forget, your best friend is your body. And it needs you!  
> So tonight, drop and give me twenty push-ups.

Back when I was in school, he managed to talk me into shifting my ass — we were required to use him. These days he hangs around in my apps and his smile drives me up the wall. He's got that smug little look that makes me grind my teeth, like: *You don't have the balls to get started, dude. Girls prefer guys like me, dude.* It gets me down. I even wonder why I don't just uninstall him. Mostly I figure he's like a good old security blanket you don't want to get rid of despite the smell. He's part of us. He followed us our whole childhood. We can't part with him anymore.

***Aymeric***  
> Remind me next week.

***PumpThatBody***  
> Okay, dude. Setting an alarm!

So Alex and I end up sinking back into our social feeds. The authenticity of the real never lasts very long. On the other side of my eye, my news feeds are stirring: tonight my *Smasher* group has planned a raid on an abandoned house in the old town. The goal is to blow off steam by smashing stuff, to clear out the accumulated stress and all the frustration this world inspires in us. We do it with baseball bats and sledgehammers. It's more authentic and it feels amazing. The meet-up spot is a good hour's walk away; I need to be home by 9 if I want to take part.  
Suddenly Alex slams on the brakes and jumps up and down like a kid.  
"Aym'! There's the goddamn ad for the VR Bar Rockoco Banana."  
"What?"  
"It's the first clue, man."  
He points at a huge gorilla — a virtual tag — shredding an electric guitar. Notes float above the character's head and he looks me dead in the eye the way every ad does. Very eye-catching, when in reality we all struggle to look at other humans that way.  
"So what do we do?" I ask.  
"We gotta swipe a coconut off him."  
"Off to a great start…"  
I scratch my head.  
"What are these guys smoking."  
Alex shrugs, then steps up to tear off the coveted object. He passes his hand through the trembling virtual halo. The gorilla lets out a few notes, then the fruit detaches and sticks to my buddy. We've seen better graphics, but Alex is happy. He raises his arm to signal his victory. He really takes this for a game. His newly acquired coconut fades out before an AR notification appears over his head.

***Gus***  
> Your friend Alex earns the badge "I've Got Big Coconuts".  
> 2 credits off your bill!  
> Valid once only.

"Damn, this is too perfect!" Alex crows.  
"You still want to go all the way?" I try, to gauge his doubts.  
"Totally, man."  
He's a real Energizer bunny. Going to be hard to stop him. He explains:  
"The dealer should be somewhere further down this alley, normally."  
I look off into the distance, but nothing comes tickling my retina.  
"He sells real *natural* fruit."  
I catch the nuance he's trying to add to that line. When I was a kid, my grandmother used to give me an apple at Christmas. I only understood much later that she was giving me something precious. To me it was just a sweet thing that took serious effort to enjoy. The seeds, the skin, the crunch going to my head, it was unpleasant. I much preferred the soy sweets the printers churn out. Then one day, seeing the price of a banana, an apple, an orange, I understood that each of those fruits was worth a week's wages.  
Alex picks his explanation back up:  
"I've gotta ask him for a couple of bananas and a cabbage."  
"What do you cook with that?"  
"Nothing! After that, he's supposed to offer me a rum-alcohol extract, something basic, to flavor my bananas and turn them into a dessert."  
"Your thing's getting elaborate."  
"You're right, we're not gonna trash real fruit. It's sacred!"  
"Might as well head home then, no?"  
Alex isn't listening to me. He's slipping through my fingers. His attention has shifted to this famous dealer who's just appeared from behind a trash can. The man projects a blue djellaba over his clothes and his surroundings suddenly take on the look of a Middle-Eastern children's tale. I can't believe it. An AR camel is lying beside his stall, where fruit is impeccably displayed. Alex steps up and starts the conversation. The vendor jabbers a few words in a heavy Arabic accent. He wants to haggle over prices. I don't believe this. He pushes the realism a bit far. Inevitably, the man ends up offering the rum extract. Alex accepts. Like he said, he goes all the way. A big grin splits his face as he holds out the seventy credits in virtual form. The bills fade from his hand into the dealer's. At that price, Alex is going to savor them, his banana and his cabbage; that covers two weeks of my monthly spending, and he doesn't earn any more than me.  
"You coming?" I call to him.  
I barely have time to catch his delighted face as he turns toward me before my AR vision narrows and blurs. Smells like trouble. A red dialog box appears in front of me. It fills my whole field of vision and blinks like it's hammering the final nail into my coffin.

***Bayeux II Police***  
> You are under arrest.  
> Please remain calm pending your apprehension.

OK!  
No joke, I think we just got nabbed... I'm not looking for trouble. I crouch down, hands behind my head. It's not the first time this kind of nonsense has happened to me and, with a bit of luck, my fine won't be too steep. After all, I didn't buy or consume anything, but Alex…  
"Damn it! Run, Aym," I hear.  
What an idiot! I tried it once when I was younger. It's truly the worst idea. For one, you can't see a thing, but on top of that you take a refusal-to-comply charge to the teeth: free tetany. It's like brambles flooding your veins and your mind getting locked in a black box. Hell for 5 seconds. And that's exactly what happens. I hear Alex's few struggling steps, his grunts, then the dull thud of his body hitting the asphalt. They're trigger-happy, the cops. Alex is going to hurt bad when he wakes up. Then again, I was only fifteen back then and my offense was as minor as I was. Four weeks of community service for the Town Hall, redoing the asphalt on the streets, helping out the garbage collectors. The high life, compared to what's waiting for Alex.  
Him, he's looking at prison. *Dream* is expensive.
