# Log #5

As my wits come back to me, I feel a hand resting on my leg.  
"Honestly," I hear, I thought you were a goner.  
A woman's voice welcomes me into this new world.  
"You were in a sorry state," she goes on.  
My sight still blurred, I try to sit up, but a hand stops me.  
"Wait a few more moments; your nanomachines are finishing the job."  
I accept without protest. I rub my eyelids. We're no longer behind the counter; the ceiling has changed. I make out tiles full of damp stains and the lingering smell reminds me of a gym locker room. The glow of the white neon lights wins out over the announcements still covering the walls. My retina stings. I shield it with an arm. I can hardly believe it can move so easily. In my back, a pain wrings a grimace out of me. I understand that I've been laid out on a bench and the slats are digging into my sides. Even so, I want to thank the one who saved me. My throat clenches. I try to move my lips.  
I feel like I'm swallowing my tongue.  
"Stay still, I'll bring you some water!"  
Her hand leaves me. I take the chance to run a diagnostic.

***Gus***  
> Hello Aymeric.  
> I nearly lost you for good.  
> You earn the badge "I Saw the Light"  
> But now, everything's fine.

***Aymeric***  
> I, uh… That's good, right?  
> Run a diagnostic

***Diagnostic***  
> Vital signs stabilized by auto-injector.  
> Summary:

- 9 fractured bones: under repair  
- 26 contusions: treatment being finalized  
- 13 lacerations: repaired  
- Presence of a benign foreign body in the intestines

> Warning: bone structures still fragile.  
> Five hours of rest recommended. Plan a full checkup at the hospital.

Honestly, auto-injectors are seriously classy!  
According to Gus, three hours ago I was about to die, and now here I am ready to stand on my feet. Some buddies had already had this heady experience, but for me it's a first. You're supposed to keep one of these syringes at home in case of emergency. Me, I never had enough cash to get one. Along with a custom bracelet, a designer skin, and a 40-square-meter flat, the injector is part of the must-have kit once you hit thirty. All things considered, I'm pretty glad the police are issued them by default. The pain's gone now. As for the fatigue, it's like I'm able to feel the nanomachines' effects.  
I'm coming back to life.  
"Here, drink this!"  
The hand slides behind my head and invites me to take a first sip. I need more. So I grab the cup and finish off its contents without stopping.  
"Thirsty, were you! my benefactor remarks, surprised."  
I swallow, then catch my breath.  
"You have no idea…"  
My using "tu" with her seems to throw her off. She grimaces, then says:  
"Even during my military service, I never got banged up this badly."  
I sit up for real this time. My head's spinning.  
"Can I have another?"  
I hand her back the cup. When she grabs it and stands, her stern look chills me. I quickly realize it's not meant for me. She's just got to learn to handle all these events that are beyond us. I try to keep the visions of horror as far away as possible, but… Damn it! I think back to Alex, to his skull caving in and the blood that doesn't seem to want to stop flowing. Shit! He was a little punk, but I liked him. I clench my teeth. I silence the rage rising in me. This cop has probably seen colleagues die without being able to do anything about it. In any case, her face bears witness to the violence she's been through; her tan skin is spattered with blood right up into her short-cropped hair. Oddly, her outfit is spotless. She must have just put it on. It's a model of augmented armor in navy blue used by the riot police, a kind of exoskeleton. Its base frame stays supple — it hugs the body's shapes — but it's also made of sturdier parts: a laminated dorsal shell running along the spine, a breastplate protecting the torso while ensuring good ventral mobility, and articulated plates covering the limbs. Stamped front and back, the word *Police* brings back bad memories. Finally, a full-face helmet with a transparent visor and padded gloves complete the set. The young woman wears them on her belt. She's not very tall, but she looks sturdy. And all this gear adds to that impression. I'm happy to believe her when she says she did her military service; I look like a child next to her.  
"There!" she announces, coming back from a water fountain.  
Her hands tremble as she hands me my drink.  
"I'm not used to saying it, but… thanks."  
"I only did my duty."  
Her eyebrows knit, then she adds:  
"You got very lucky, that's all."  
Off in the distance, a scream reminds me the situation isn't resolved.  
"We're going to have to start getting ready to move out. I have a mission to carry out."  
"Wait, wait! I've just come back to life and you—"  
My brain suddenly catches up with what she just said.  
"What? A mission? What mission? I don't do missions."  
"We don't have much time."  
"Time for what? Aren't we well protected here?"  
My throat knots before I add:  
"Tell me we're well protected here!"  
Her look hardens and she slips her arm under mine to force me to my feet.  
"Hey, easy!" I protest.  
I'm seeing things. After zombies, now robot women!  
"No time," I said! We gear up and get out of here.  
With the tip of her finger, she points to a set of armor like her own, carefully laid out on a table meant for it. I step toward it, shaky, and finally realize we're in the middle of a big locker room. Tall black lockers surround us. Most seem to have been forced open, their contents dumped on the floor. Traces of blood foul the tiles and wind their way to a door barricaded with a heap of corpses. I shake my head. If this woman's the one who knocked out this whole crowd, I'll happily take the same armor. In this never-ending nightmare, with this full-body armor, a childhood dream is coming true, the kind you don't admit to your buddies at the risk of looking like a total dork.  
"But, how does this go on, anyway?"  
The cop doesn't answer me. She's far too busy checking her AR interface. Her arms flail every which way and a dissatisfied smirk forms on her lips.  
"Damn," she lets slip.  
Seeing I still haven't started getting dressed, she loses patience.  
"Maybe this will motivate you to move your ass…"  
Her hand whips through the air in my direction.

***Gus***  
> F. Nakach is offering you a screen share. Do you accept?

***Aymeric***  
> I accept.

A video suddenly appears in my field of vision. The header indicates it's a feed from an internal police station camera. You can see one of the creatures, but this one doesn't look like the ones I've already come across. A tag floats above its head: *Mike Tesnov*. Ouch! That's the famous Mike? He really does have some big problems with his arms. The first is their length: they touch the floor. The second is their bulk: they're as thick as three of my thighs combined. And he's found a funny way to use them. He moves like a gorilla. Looks like his body didn't take kindly to our new surprise update. His drawn-and-quartered bodybuilder physique doesn't make me want to cross his path, and I'm not even talking about his behavior. He's edgy, Mike. He pounds anything within reach without thinking. In fact, another hysteric pays the price. Mike's fist swings out, smashes into his victim, and embeds him in a wall. The plaster cracks. The body bursts like an old pumpkin. Apparently Mike has a thing for cave paintings, but that's not all. Now he stops in front of his new work of art and stares at it. What's he doing? Waiting, it seems. My companion tells me to watch the next part carefully. I don't understand, but I focus. After a few seconds, a strange phenomenon occurs. My eyes go wide. Part of the corpse's blood spray comes to life. It swells at first, then its surface stretches into a myriad of thin red filaments that float over to Mike and penetrate his body.  
"What the hell is this!" I let slip. "That's insane…"  
The cop lets out a disapproving sigh.  
"I'm not sure I know what's happening to him…"  
Then she rubs her skull before continuing:  
"Just yesterday Mike was serving coffee and shining the section chief's shoes. Now it's clear that's not Mike anymore, it's something else. I watched him while you were recovering. He methodically cleared out the first floor, and every time, he did that thing. Looks like he feeds. At first his arms weren't that big, or anything. I don't even know if we'll be able to fight him if he finds us…"  
A shiver runs down my spine.  
"This guy's gonna wreck us, yeah..."  
The flimsy barricade protecting us won't hold. So I pick up the pace and try to pull on the various pieces of the augmented armor without really knowing where to start. A real puzzle, actually! Seeing me flounder like a rookie, my new teammate helps me out while keeping an eye on the video.  
"I don't know how many of these creatures are left on our floor," she says, tightening a strap on my back. I also don't know if Mike will decide to go up or down, but I figure if he chooses to come down, it won't take him more than five minutes to dig us out. And no more than one to gut us.  
I swallow. She really knows how to put it nicely to motivate me.  
"This version of Mike is pretty efficient."  
"You're telling me."  
When I'm finally ready, my companion checks the fastenings one last time and tells me to roll my shoulders to see how the armor behaves. It feels a bit cumbersome at first, but she seems to know what she's doing. Either way, I'm not going to be running around. My bones still need to strengthen up a bit more.  
A necessity suddenly comes to mind.  
"Any chance of getting something to defend myself with?"  
The cop hesitates a second before answering.  
"You're not actually planning to try to face that thing, are you?"  
"No, no, that's not what I mean. It's just for outside..."  
She thinks it over. I press:  
"It'd put me at ease."  
"In that case, I'll unlock the armory for you…"  
She points to a door behind us. I think I've annoyed her a bit.  
"Take what you want, but don't weigh yourself down. We're not here to play commando. We're going straight to the emergency rally point."  
I confirm with a nod. Then I attempt a step. My leg sketches out such a slow, heavy movement that I nearly trip. I didn't think exoskeletons took this much energy to operate. As I catch myself on a locker to make my second step, a discreet smile is born on my teammate's lips.  
"I forgot a little detail," she tells me, fingers dancing over her interface.  
Suddenly, a new pop-up appears in front of me.

***Gus***  
> An unknown device wants to connect to your bracelet.  
> Civil Protection Armor v3.47  
> Identifier: AB:78:F8:AE:14:85  
> Do you accept the interface?

***Aymeric***  
> Obviously I accept, Gus…

Right away, fans set into my thighs kick in and their startup comes with a series of beeps. I feel like I'm booting up the kind of old clunker they used back in the 21st century, the kind you sometimes dig up at flea markets. I knew the police were that short on funding. In front of me, Gus busies himself. He tells me he's installing the control module. I watch the progress bar fill at top speed, then an AR readout appears in the corner of my eye. It's several gauges giving me the overall state of my armor and the available power. On top of that, a pictogram tells me the built-in auto-injector is operational. Even if the graphics aren't the freshest, I find it pretty cool. But what rules even more is that when I take a step, the exoskeleton assists me. I feel like I'm on a little cloud. Like I'm jogging naked on the moon.  
As I get used to the first few steps, I ask:  
"How much does it weigh?"  
"Fifty kilos or so, at most."  
A goofy smile stretches my lips as I head for the armory. There, I come upon a windowless room whose walls are lined with display cases. They hold an impressive collection of toys for big brutes. My eyes sparkle at these weapons that populate my video games but that I've never had the chance to lay hands on. Unfortunately, when I try to access them, the doors stay shut and an AR message tells me I need a prefectural authorization. I grind my teeth. Same with the smaller calibers. So frustrating! I'm starting to wonder why this cop sold me a dream by giving me the right to gear up. Disillusioned with the situation, I grab the first things that come to hand: a heavy baton, closer to a baseball bat than a rolling pin, plus an impulse pistol. I weigh it all up. The hysterics are going to get an earful with this, but it's for sure we won't be stopping King Kong.  
"What's she so scared of, anyway?" I mutter. "Hurting them?"  
I grumble a bit, then I pass in front of a mirror on my way out. I stop. I really don't look good. The blood on my face, my hands. Without my favorite skin, I have a hard time recognizing myself. You quickly lose the habit of seeing your own face. It even gets hard to meet the gaze of this tan-skinned guy who looks like he's someone else entirely. He's got a slightly pockmarked face and unruly hair. He's clearly not at his best. I sigh, wiping my cheeks with the back of my hand, then I pull on my helmet to hide the mop. I leave my visor open. With all this gear, I better understand why the *nons* are scared of cops. And it's true I look like a machine ready to eradicate humanity: *exterminate*, *annihilate*, *destroy*.

***Gus***  
> Fara Nakach invites you to a private conversation.  
> Do you accept the invitation?

***Aymeric***  
> Yes, I accept.

A chat window appears at once.

***Fara***  
> I took the liberty of inviting you.  
> It'll be more practical if we lose sight of each other.

***Aymeric***  
> Yeah, true.

My reply is a bit terse; I've always found it strange to talk by messaging with someone in the next room, all the more so when that person is a cop. And then this sudden intimacy unsettles me.

***Fara***  
> What are you waiting for? We're leaving soon.

She takes things very seriously, but she's right. I'm a poor civilian wasting her time, I get the feeling, and Mike isn't going to wait for me to finish my shopping spree before he shows up and turns us into a bloody mary.  
"Coming," I call from the armory.  
Then I roll in like a tourist. She looks me up and down, her helmet on her head.

***Fara***  
> Use the channel from now on; we need to stay discreet.

I nod, a little embarrassed. I'm a real little soldier now.

***Fara***  
> For now, we need to reach the emergency bunker.

***Aymeric***  
> Uh… Right.  
> Wouldn't it be smarter to get out of the city?

My teammate closes her eyes, brings a hand to her forehead, and sighs.

***Fara***  
> I'm going to pretend you didn't say that. Let's pick back up…  
> The bunker is under the Town Hall, that's where we'll be able to learn more about the situation. It's also the rally point we use in case of emergency, even if, I'll admit, nobody expected this...

Her hands tremble. She studies them for a second before going on.

***Fara***  
> I understand you're worried, but believe me, there's no safer place than the bunker. Then again, I can let you fend for yourself. It's your right to die stupidly. As for me, now that you've got something to defend yourself with, I consider my duty done.

Her look is cold. She's starting to scare me and I don't know what I could have done to put her in such a mood. Then again, I get it: I'm having a hard time coping too; I'd like it to stop too; I want to forget the seriously nasty stuff that's surely going to haunt me for a while too. But I'm not bothering anyone, you know. It's crazy! I just hope she'll understand that and won't abandon me after a nervous breakdown.  
As I'm chewing over her reaction, I think that in my lack of an answer and my crumbling face, she picks up on my distress.  
Her mouth twists into a grimace, then she blows out a big breath.

***Fara***  
> Sorry...  
> I'm in a hurry for all this to be over.

I watch her take a deep breath, then she switches back to briefing mode. When I say this cop is a robot, I'm not joking!

***Fara***  
> So...  
> I've been able to scope out the immediate surroundings of the station: it's chaos in the city. So, if you're willing, I could use a second pair of arms and eyes. I might have access to the nearest security cameras, but that won't be enough. It's a partnership I'm proposing. You think we can do this, the two of us?

I don't have time to answer her before a roar passes through the room. The walls vibrate and I feel my guts contract. I feel like someone put my head under a bell and struck it hard. Fara holds her forehead. She staggers a second before plunging her eyes into mine. Her face is pale. Between us, flakes of plaster fall from the ceiling. I understand it's Mike announcing his arrival with a roar so powerful I didn't think it possible.  
As we collect ourselves, Fara examines her interface.  
"Damn!" she lets slip. He's heading our way.  
And as she starts pulling me away from here, higher-pitched cries ring out.

***Fara***  
> He's got plenty to keep him busy with the creatures that are left...  
> We can get a head start if we go through the basement. Plus, I've got access to the motorbikes. Even if they'll suffer under the weight of the armor, we'll be able to shake him and reach the bunker faster. You with me?

Of course I'm with her! After the exoskeleton, the motorbike: it's Christmas come early. I shouldn't get excited over so little, but when you come within an inch of death, you've got to know how to bite into life like a 50-credit apple. So I set off limping after Fara, who plunges into a corridor behind the locker room. Creatures came through here before us. They left disfigured corpses in their wake, torn-off limbs and pools of blood. I pass in front of broken windows. Outside, the red glow of the announcements reflects off a sky covered in smoke. Strong fumes of burnt plastic rise from a flaming trash can. They mingle with the ones escaping a burning corpse. I'm glad I didn't eat anything this morning. Under the streetlights, the shapeless shadows of hysterics in the smoke chase each other, tear at each other, and yelp like wild animals.  
The stairwell isn't far now.  
Fara stops suddenly. She looks behind me. At the other end of the corridor, through an open door, I catch a few flashes followed by sparks. Sounds of impacts reach us and the limp body of a creature is hurled through the doorframe. It rips apart on impact and splatters the tiles with its fluids. I feel a hand on my arm; Fara pulls me toward the stairs.
