# Log #3

Everyone holds their breath. It's dead silent in the room. Even me, I wait with dread through the few seconds the restart needs, and on my retina lingers the afterimage of the Groogle logo. In a flash, it finally disappears. The AR reactivates. It's like a near-imperceptible veil settling over reality. Before I can access my account, a strange message appears:

***Groogle NeXT***  
> Update %3µ4$3EF complete

***Gus***  
> Initializing … Error  
> Initializing … Error  
> Initializing … Error  
> Failsafe boot protocol activated.  
> Initializing … OK  
> Hello Aymeric, I am your personal assistant.  
> Alert: communication with the nanomachines interrupted.  
> Reconnecting … In progress  
> System archives detected.  
> Recovering archives … OK  
> You have 376 messages pending across your various social networks.  
> You have 8 personal messages.

In the room, a few forced laughs catch me off guard.  
"I think some intern's about to get fired!" I hear.  
So it's a glitch, then? And so, as if to release the pressure, a few detainees start pounding their fists on the glass while others cackle their heads off. I don't really know what to think, but I end up relaxing too. Everyone seems to be celebrating. It's not another restriction to add to all the ones already imprisoning us, it's not a surprise law forced through while everyone was looking away, it's a simple glitch. Among the cops, they're breathing easier too. They're even slapping each other on the back to reassure themselves, the kind of slap that'd dislocate my shoulder blade given how scrawny I am. Beside me, Alex finds the strength to laugh too. He's even crying. He wipes his tears with the back of his hands like a child. I've never seen him so emotional. He's drooling and snot is running from his nose. I frown. After his tetany, I'm starting to worry about his mental state. So I step up to the plexi and call out to him:  
"Calm down, buddy! It's going to be okay! It's over."  
In response, his eyes go red and now he's staring at me. He's not crying anymore. His attention is fixed on me. I back off in a reflex motion. He's clearly got a problem. My gut tells me to run. I don't know why, but Alex just went from child to beast in a second. And that beast throws itself, headfirst, against the wall between us. When he smashes into the plexi, I fall backward, dumbstruck. My eyes go wide. A scream leaves my mouth like it doesn't belong to me. I don't understand what's happening anymore. *Alex*! My lips tremble while my gaze can't tear itself from the blood spilling out of his forehead and running down the glass.  
"Damn it Alex… What… what are you doing to me here?"  
I pull myself together as I see him slump to one side; I have to react.  
"Help, damn it!"  
My legs are wobbling, but I manage to straighten up.  
"Help!"  
I bolt for the door of my cell.  
"My buddy Alex, he's dying!"  
Nobody answers me. The laughter has stopped, giving way — I only notice it now — to screams and the dull sound of impacts on plastic. The time for relief is over. What's going on? Who can get us out of here? Through the transparent strip that gives me a view of the corridor, I make out the cops drawing their batons. They're staggering, and from their lips drips a foam like the one that escaped Alex's mouth. Their faces are going red, their veins bulge under their skin, and I see them landing blows on their own chests, heads, legs. Like they're hitting at random. It makes no sense to me and I stay glued to this improbable scene, but an impact on my right tears another scream out of me. My neighbor's losing it against the wall too. I'm shaking. My head is spinning while Gus throws a whole series of readouts into a corner of my retina.

***Gus***  
> Alert: elevated heart rate  
> Alert: built-in countermeasures ineffective  
> Alert: communication with the nanomachines interrupted.  
> Attempting reconnection … In progress

"Goddammit," I let slip through clenched teeth.  
Is everyone going crazy or what? A burning sensation devours me from the inside. My vision blurs. My temples explode. Am I going to end up like them? I don't want to end up like them. A lump blocks my throat. My breathing cuts out. I feel myself falling, but I catch myself on the wall. I think I'm dreaming!

***Gus***  
> Alert: intense stress detected.  
> Nanomachines inoperative: launching the Serenity program.

***Serenity***  
> Hello Aymeric, please follow these instructions in rhythm.  
> Breathe in. Breathe out. Breathe in. Breathe out.  
> Picture a beach where the waves tickle your feet.

"For God's sake!" I scream, while Gus thinks he's helping.  
Even so, I listen to him. I exhale. I try to picture it even as the racket goes on. It's hard, but I exhale. I exhale again. And even though I put all my goodwill into the effort, nothing works. The warm sandy beach stays out of reach of my imagination. Especially since an alarm goes off without warning and now drowns the surrounding cacophony under its shrill notes. My heart's going to explode. I feel it pounding like a devil in my chest. My hands clamp over my ears. I curl up in a corner of my cell. I'm scared. I can't believe this.

***Bayeux II Police***  
> Your ...  
> Your Attention ple …  
> The station will be closing soon.  
> The station is going to...

As the announcements multiply, spiral out, and break off, I press myself behind my bench, trying not to look at Alex's blood still tracing its path down the wall. I feel like throwing up. I want it to stop. The ceiling lights flicker like a strobe. You'd think you were in a nightclub when the DJ botches the sample and everyone yells at him. The universe is going completely off the rails. I tense up. I close my eyes, but my eyelids aren't enough to protect me. I want someone to get me out of here. I want to stay alive. My blood is burning; the anger rises. I scream. I scream so hard my throat catches fire, like an animal, without a word, with everything I have left of my will. I scream and I don't even hear myself screaming. I scream and the alarm finally falls silent. I'm dazed, drained.  
Suddenly, a familiar sound pulls me out of my collapse. I thought I'd given up, but my body remobilizes. I quickly understand that, if I don't react, what's about to happen to me won't please me at all: the cell doors have just unlocked. A few detainees have already noticed. They're escaping. Their gait is erratic. Their limbs don't all respond at the same time. If my life weren't on the line, I'd probably be laughing, but right now I just want them to stay far away from me. In fact, I see one of them coming closer. He stumbles over himself, gets up, runs before he walks, then charges headfirst again into the middle of the cops who keep clubbing themselves. The latter don't react right away to the aggression. It takes them a good five seconds before they throw themselves on him, bellowing their fury like a chimpanzee whose favorite toy's been confiscated. I shake my head. I'm seeing things. I seem to be the only person still conscious here and I don't know whether to thank the heavens for this miracle.  
I stay gaping.  
"But… zombies went out of style over seventy years ago."  
All at once, I notice my door is creeping open.  
"Oh no, you stay shut!"  
The trouble is, it swings outward, there's no handle on my side, and I'm far too attached to my ten fingers to leave them out in the open. I grind my teeth. I see nothing but my jacket to help me. I take it off. I look even more vulnerable in a white tank top. I really don't have the build for this kind of adventure. Damn it! So I take a big breath. My courage comes back.  
I'm going. I take the risk.  
A glance confirms that nobody's interested in me. My two hands slip out through the gap. They're trembling, but I still grab the latch on the other side with my jacket, then tie it in a knot, pull toward me like a madman, and brace myself near the bench to hold it shut if anyone tries to approach. I hope my strength will be enough to deter them — nothing's less certain — and, in front of me, the horror goes on: people are fighting with batons, fists, and feet. Sometimes they bite, claw, or take their fury out on those already on the ground, whose stillness leaves no doubt about their condition. The cries and the screams tear at my insides. None of these behaviors are human anymore and even the bodies are starting to contort in impossible ways.  
"Damn it!" I let slip.  
Off in the distance, I notice two detainees who seem to have avoided the worst as well. The first is in shock. She hasn't managed to get past the larva state that had me curling up in a corner. The second stays alert. He's standing straight as a post in the middle of his cell. He's watching the scene and, if he hadn't been so calm, given his filthy look and his torn clothes, I might have mistaken him for one of them. I don't really know what he's doing. Looks like he's waiting for the right moment to act. I wave a hand at them. Neither of them notices me.

***Gus***  
> Alert: communication with the nanomachines interrupted.  
> Connection with the nanomachines… Failed.  
> Factory reset protocol.  
> Initializing … OK  
> Connection with the nanomachines … OK  
> Calibrating… In progress  
> You have 398 messages pending across your various social networks.  
> You have 8 personal messages.

Gus is working himself up. I don't know what's gotten into him, trying stuff, but it's really not reassuring. That said, he's managed to reconnect to my nanomachines. We'll take that as a first win. And then there are all those pending messages I still haven't checked. I exhale. I'm scared of what I'll find, but maybe hidden among them is some crucial piece of information. With a simple blink, I open the whole batch of content and scroll through it without dwelling too much on the images and videos going around. I can't help noticing that most of the messages deal with this marvelous, astonishing update. Wouldn't expect otherwise, really. It's kind of the buzz of the century right now. In fact, the national social network is announcing a historic record for the hashtag: SnitchYourUpdate. It's scary, but not as scary as what you find on the Bayeux II feed, which expresses its surprise in a four-hundred-character statement and urges the population to stay calm.  
This is insane! We're all done for, is that it? I feel the sweat running down my back. I lose my calm once again. I need to know what's happening outside. I can't do like my neighbor and curl up into a dream hoping it'll pass. I don't want to close my eyes. So I pull up the latest news, the stuff from less than a minute ago. The first images have barely loaded before I'm already regretting it. Everywhere, people are screaming and attacking anything within reach. Everywhere, the corpses of innocents in the streets and the same inhuman behaviors. I can't take any more. My heart is decomposing in my chest.  
Everything's turning into a nightmare.  
Behind the wall, the brawl is in full swing. Nails and claws have torn into flesh that's bleeding heavily. I see broken arms, twisted legs. My face splits into a grimace when the coppery smell of blood seeps into my cell. Some bodies won't get up again, and the fluids are spreading all the way to me. In front of my door, a young man lies on the floor, his skull split in two. I can't imagine the force it took to get to that point, but his death must have been quick. My throat tightens. I feel spasms shaking my stomach. I keep myself from throwing up. Everything seems absurd to me. I don't know what to hold on to. Is it really worth busting my ass to keep these monsters from coming to rip my face off? I think I'm about to give up, but a blow on the plexi snaps me back to reality.  
I'm still alive for now…  
And, just like that other detainee waiting patiently, I'm going to wait for an opportunity to come up. When it does, I'll risk it all. It's all I've got left to get out of this. I close my eyes, then I open them again. The world hasn't changed in the meantime, but here comes my opportunity, served up on a silver platter: a cop just dropped dead a few steps from my cell. He's going to have a hard time getting up. His hand and the rest of his arm are still firmly attached to his baton, but not really to the rest of his body anymore.  
There we go, I've found how to do it. A cramp grips my guts. Like they want to forbid me from even thinking about it: *It's a crap idea, Aym'!*  
"Go to hell!"  
I feel it. There are moments like this in life when you have to dive in and show courage like the guys on the front lines who always end up skewered anyway, but who, for a reason I'd never understood until now, still put themselves on the front lines. That's it! This time, I'm the one on the front line. So I scout the route. Between my cell and the exit door, there are only four people left. The others are busy devouring each other or wandering in circles, aimless. Yeah, it's doable!  
I breathe in. I breathe out.  
Gus is going to be pleased to see how I've mastered his exercises now.  
"Who's your daddy!" I shout to pump myself up.  
No time to feel embarrassed, I motivate myself however I can. A heat takes hold of my chest and my head. I breathe in. I breathe out. I don't fight it. This adrenaline surge is going to give me the strength to do what I have to do. I breathe in. I breathe out. Gus is panicking, but I pay it no attention. A creature crosses the corridor. I wait. It pounces on another cop facing my cell. With a big shoulder shove, I push the door to knock them over. They collapse. Quickly, a scrum forms around them. I take advantage of it. I throw myself forward through the opening and grab the baton, armed with its owner's arm, which still won't detach from it. A wrist strap holds the two together. Damn it! While I'm struggling to separate them, one of the enraged spots me. He's got an ear between his teeth. A nervous giggle escapes my mouth as he steps clumsily toward me. His face is swollen, like it's bruised, his skin cracking in places — looks like it's about to burst — and his bloody hands terrify me. At the tips of his ten fingers, his nails have taken on the look of short claws. I shake my head. I panic. I drop the idea of detaching this damn baton and, when the creature lunges at me, I just use the whole arm. Here we go! The blow lands straight in his face. The impact is brutal. I scream as I hear the bones crack. The weapon slips out of my hands, then a pain in my lower back catches me by surprise. I'm flung into the brawl. A laugh behind me. I realize it's the bastard who was patiently waiting for the right moment. He found it by using me as a diversion. What a snake!  
I'm seeing red. My vision narrows. My teeth clench so hard I think my jaw could split my skull. I can no longer make out anything but lights flashing like white, black, and red lightning. The inhuman voices wash over me. The alarms run me through. All this suffering blends with the coppery taste of blood running into my mouth. This melody intoxicates me. For an instant, I think of nothing but my own survival and my one goal: getting out of here.
