# Log #8

Gus drowns me under notifications while my proximity detector goes wild. I can't stay here. I get back up. I force myself. Big tears roll down my cheeks. I sniffle. I can't even wipe my face with all the gear on my back. Time to flee, even if I've got snot on my nose. I run. I think back to the sound of the neck snapping on the steps. It chases me. My chest hurts as I cross the street and head down the first lane that comes. It's darker here. My helmet switches straight to night vision. No corpse, no creature to jump me. I'm alone and safe, yet my whole body won't stop shaking. I try to catch my breath.

Warning: keep this up and you expose yourself to more serious fractures!

***Aymeric***  
> Shut up!

I can't take it anymore: I snap.  
I'm going to wreck these crap apps and their designers. Did it never occur to them that during a crisis is not the time to tell me my heart's beating too fast, that I need sleep, or that I'm overdoing the exercise? Seriously! I know it perfectly well. I feel it. I tense up with anger. I want to scream, to have a good cry too, and to curl up in a ball. My legs hurt, my ribs. Everywhere! I feel like a goddamn piñata, except it's not candy these damn hysterics are after. They're after my life. So damn annoying! And me, what did I do? It's driving me nuts. While I don't really remember much of what happened at the police station, just now I was fully aware I was smashing that poor girl's spine. I feel like throwing up. I'd never have believed killing was so easy. Sure, we like hitting things, us *Smashers*, we knock each other on the side of the head between buddies now and then, and we take it out on useless old buildings, but we never beat up anyone, not even the *nons*. Today, I killed. I killed a woman and two men, and on top of that, I felt it did me good…  
A message from Fara suddenly pulls me out of my lamenting.

***Fara***  
> I know what happened, Aymeric. Your personal assistant told me.  
> I don't answer; she goes on.

***Fara***  
> I'm not going to congratulate you, nor condemn you. And I can't say I fully understand what you're feeling either, but I went through it too during the climate exodus. They were people like you and me at the start, but they forgot what a life was worth, preying on those fleeing their countries. They had that same urge to kill in their eyes. Cruelty serves no purpose, but you have to know how to use force. You were strong. You survived…

***Aymeric***  
> Leave me alone.

***Fara***  
> Stop being childish…

"My childishness?" I mutter, without sending it.

***Fara***  
> As planned, we meet at the Saturn 8, but I won't wait for you there more than an hour if you drag your feet. I'm already behind enough! I'll try to stay within comms range, but I'm not promising anything. Good luck!

On the *Hide-and-Seek* interface, Fara's green dot drifts inexorably away. I'm scared of losing it. I'm scared of being alone for good. My body's heavy, but it gets up. I grimace. In front of me, it's still just as dark. The streetlamp light barely reaches the few vehicles stopped in the middle of the road. That's when I understand where I've landed. I'm not in a pedestrian alley — no — I took one of the access lanes that cut through the blocks of houses and lead to the private parking lots under the buildings. That's where they store the self-service autonomous cars, that's also where they come to recharge their batteries on their own. Two of them serve as my shelter. Behind the windshield of the first, I make out the lifeless shape of a person. Nothing monstrous about them. They look asleep. Yet Gus tells me they're disconnected. In other words, the update killed them on the spot. I sigh; some got lucky. Then an alert suddenly pulls me from my gloom.

***Gus***  
> You can't stay here, Aymeric.  
> This lane is forbidden to pedestrians: Please exit!

***Aymeric***  
> Damn it, Gus!  
> It's the Apocalypse and you're worried about that?  
> You really need to update yourself a bit, you poor thing.

***Gus***  
> Update impossible: Internet connection severed.  
> Please leave this lane!

There are days when I want to fry this Gus, but I quickly remember that without him I'd be totally lost. So, despite myself, I take to the road again, grumbling, baton in hand. Since the internet's gone down, all the vehicles meant to bring their passengers safely home are stuck. Before reaching the street, I pass a good number of them. I feel like I'm crossing a desecrated cemetery. Most of the *metal tombs* are broken open from the inside: smashed windows, shreds of bloodstained clothing, dark smears, and gutted corpses laid out on the shoulder. I shudder. The radar shows a creature less than five meters away. I see it watching me from inside an autonomous car. I keep a safe distance, but as I pass nearby, its eyes devour me. Despite its need for fresh flesh, it looks harmless. Its body seems to have melted. You'd think it was a skeleton draped in an old sheet. I look away. And here I thought this corner was quieter than the others; I realize that's not the case.  
I'm going to have to get used to these horrors.

***Local Communication Lounge***  
> 28 new connections to the lounge.  
> 64 people connected within a 50m radius.  
> 96 unread messages.

The lounge won't stop churning, by the way: it's crowded. I ask Gus to filter out the photos and videos. I can do without them. Reality's already unbearable enough as it is. Facing me, across the street, stand communal housing units. They're recognizable by their very peculiar layout, ultra-contemporary design straight out of the mind of a cocaine-addicted architect or that of a kid stacking heaps of bricks at random. And these bricks, actually, they're apartments glued one to another. You set one on top of another, then two, then three, offsetting and rotating them a bit each time. It takes on the look of a twisting house of cards, but it holds up better all the same. These slightly deranged buildings always frame a square with a few playgrounds for the kids and some trees. In daylight, you can even make out the greenery through the gaps between the concrete blocks. Here, in the dead of night, with the streetlamps faltering, it looks like there's nothing but the void behind this anarchic rampart. And then, to add to the chaos, the AR coating that camouflages the buildings' natural gray is going completely haywire. It won't display beyond fifty meters and keeps projecting the government alert. I hope it'll settle down soon; I can't take all this red assaulting my retina anymore.  
I try to stay on my guard. The alleys are trenches and the streets war zones. My gaze sweeps the facades when the movement of a banner catches my attention. It's tied to the balcony of one of the top floors of the communal housing. Right away, I ask Gus to zoom. A virtual window appears to help me aim. It's nothing but a blanket. I zoom in a bit more. A message has been written on the fabric. I can read: *Help*!  
I narrow my eyes, skeptical. The last person who asked for help ended her days with her skull smashed on a staircase. I won't be caught twice. And besides, Fara keeps drifting away. She'll soon be out of my range; I hesitate. The creatures might be capable, through some kind of old reflex, of communicating on the common lounge, but given their intellectual abilities, I doubt they use schemes that elaborate to turn us into a four o'clock snack.  
I mull it over. It's almost on the way, actually.

***Aymeric***  
> Fara, sorry about earlier. I'm an idiot...

***Fara***  
> Don't say that, you're brave.  
> And I'm glad you've bounced back.

***Aymeric***  
> Thanks.  
> I'm going to have to take a slight detour, though.  
> I think someone needs help.

***Fara***  
> Be very careful.  
> I wish you good luck.

Luck, I'm going to need it. I'll have to cross this street of death, climb up into these buildings without drawing any hysteric, and come out alive. I don't know what's gotten into me, really. Maybe Fara's rubbing off on me. Or maybe it's this armor. I don't know, but I'll have to go carefully. Let's start with a little reconnaissance. So I check my radar. Everything looks quiet, but most of the dots keep moving between the arcs of circles. If I want to be able to focus on potential threats, I need to clean up my interface. I minimize the useless windows, then ask Gus to apply an AR filter, a kind of big grid with metric markings. That's already not bad, but I also need an extra option. I dig through the settings. There's that thing we never turn on with our bracelets: name display. It's an AR label that floats above people within sight. It could be useful to me. I activate the whole thing. A white, translucent wave sweeps the space and spreads out fifty meters around me. Across the landscape, the grid stays discreet. It appears as thin lines. It's a bit startling; more and more, it feels like being in a video game.  
On the other hand, I don't see a single name even though the radar clearly shows a connection less than ten meters away. That worries me. Behind me, there's only the private lane leading to the underground garages, and in front, it's the street. Even if it's studded with dismembered remains scattered like wisps of straw, I still remember the way a creature got decapitated by passing too close to a heap of corpses. So better avoid getting near the bus over there, from which a mountain of lifeless bodies has spilled. I'd rather not even imagine the scenario that led to such a disaster. The distance seems to match that famous dot. Who knows what this charnel house might be hiding? I clench my teeth. I'm getting impatient. Damn, what am I waiting for to move my ass? There's nothing on the horizon. Come on! The safest thing is probably to charge straight toward one of the buildings and jump over the fence. My armor can propel me no problem. My bones, on the other hand, are going to make me suffer again. Well, too bad! The plan's made and all that's left is to carry it out.  
So, heeding nothing but my courage — and my interface's assistance — I already picture myself as a valiant knight in shining armor, saving the gorgeous princess at the mercy of her captors. I run as best I can. I believe in it. I convince myself it's fine, that I'm in no danger. I slalom between the shreds and tatters. I dodge the pools of blood, but halfway there, I stop. The shudder of metal against asphalt cuts my momentum. I'm sure one of the cars on my right just moved. I keep running, backward. I stay alert, but like a hermit crab coming out of its shell, a creature emerges from under the carcass. Its hands grip the bodywork, then it pushes up on legs as flabby as a mollusk's body. Under the engine, its shapeless head appears and an AR label suddenly shows up right above: *G. Magnard*. Now that I know its name, we'll be able to get friendly. All the more since at first glance, it doesn't look like it's after trouble. Aymeric! You're imagining things, man. If the ground's strewn with still-fresh human remains, it's surely because of your new friend. Clearly, there's no way I'm staying around here. Damn it! Now it's hoisting its burden and dragging it behind, snail-style. It's ready to set off hunting for a new lettuce leaf, and that lettuce leaf is me. A shiver runs down my spine. I now know what vegetable patches feel when a squad of slugs storms them. It speeds up. It's not with a baton that I'll get rid of this behemoth.  
I bolt. I try to, at least; this mass of flesh and iron is picking up speed. My heart races. I double my efforts to flee. And as the armor grasps my intentions and assists me, I hear a sort of barking come from who knows where, followed by a scream, then three more. As usual, all it takes is a bit of action to round up the buddies. The thing is, I'm no master of subtlety. The fence is getting closer. It's two meters high. Should be fine! At my back, heavy footsteps and the screech of the canned hermit crab are hot on my heels. I'm almost there. The fans of my helmet kick in. I suddenly feel lighter, more supple. I propel myself in one bound and land on the other side with grace and a certain pain. Gus rails against my acrobatics. I'm going to end up regretting it. I grind my teeth and glance behind me while running. The living car smashes against the fence, but the obstacle holds. What a moron! Part of its body went through the bars like through a meat grinder and its squashed flesh is still moving. It expands, contracts, then returns to its original shape. Yeck! I'm not even sure this creature suffered from the impact, but it's going to stay wedged there a while longer. I move off and, as I climb the first steps leading to the building, the wreck throws me one last guttural cry: smells like frustration.  
I'm starting to manage pretty well, no? Eh! We're still a long way from there. And barely arrived in front of the entrance lobby, I'm already starting to question again what's driving me to save my princess: someone seems to have already had the idea of gutting the bay window that serves as an airlock. The glass crunches under the weight of my armor as I slip through the gap. I reach the lobby. The ceiling is low. I feel cramped. The walls are covered in mailboxes and half of them are bashed in. They're vomiting their parcels onto the floor. I don't understand what could push someone to do that, but they must have had a grudge against the postal service, because they left traces of blood everywhere. That doesn't reassure me much, and neither does my radar. The dots have closed in on me dramatically. The building's crowded, and the banner was hanging from one of the top floors. I must be close enough to contact whoever took refuge in that apartment. So I move forward carefully to find a spot out of sight. I finally settle into the stairwell, beside the elevator. I send my message. I hope it's not too late!

***A. Bator***  
> The apartment with the banner, there!  
> If you can hear me, answer.  
> Still alive?

My call immediately gets lost in the incoherent flood of scrolling messages. I think there aren't many survivors in these high-rises. Makes me wonder how many humans survived the update. I shake my head to clear the question and send another message, quickly swallowed by the crowd. I'm being dumb. What am I afraid of, really? I can spam away happily. No moderator's going to slap me with a warning. So I let it rip while keeping an eye on my radar. There are more than fifty people in the area. A few dots tremble about twenty meters off, but most aren't moving. They're surely pacing in circles in their respective apartments, pondering their next meal. In any case, for my rescue, I think it's a bust. Nobody answers me. I send one last volley, and while I wait for it to hit or miss, I check my notifications a bit. It's dumb, but it's an old habit ingrained in our behavior for generations, I'm sure. The second we've got a free moment, our brain churns to remind us we're going to miss a music video premiere, that a friend still hasn't answered our last message, or that the government's going to sneak through new restrictive measures again. This brain should worry instead about stopping the freaking out. Result: nothing remarkable in my notifications except the usual warnings from Gus and PumpThatBody telling me my mad dash earned me a few hundredths of a point. I exhale. What's wrong with me, really? I'm wasting my time with this nonsense.  
A message on the lounge calls me to order.

***L. Humbert***  
> A. Bator, I'm here!  
> I'm alive, but my father, I don't know…  
> I can't get him out of his capsule.

*L. Humbert* must be loaded to have a personal capsule.

***A. Bator***  
> What's your apartment number?  
> I'm coming to get you.

***L. Humbert***  
> Fifth floor, apartment 503.  
> Hurry, plz!

A quick visit to her public profile confirms it's indeed a *princess*. Lou Humbert is a fifteen-year-old girl, a fan of animal AR skins and way-too-colorful hairstyles. Another sign that her parents managed, one way or another, to climb the rungs of our society.

***A. Bator***  
> Omw!

All that's left is to reach the fifth floor unharmed. The upside is that once past the entrance lobby, you arrive in a space open onto the square. The sounds from the street no longer reach me and the darkness reigning here doesn't scare me anymore; my helmet knows how to handle it. On the facades, neon lights faintly illuminate a whole network of walkways and stairs serving the various apartments. From here, I see no one, even though my radar's still going off just as much. I cut the metric grid — I won't need it — and follow the route that seems safest to me, as discreetly as possible. Even if the architecture's a bit convoluted, I reach the relevant floor without difficulty, the top one, in front of door 510. Lou lives slightly further on. Everything's so quiet. I hear my breathing and my heart pounding my chest. Gus is silent too. I stop for a second. From here, you can see the red glow of the sky. The city is slowly burning down. Looks like it's pretty much the same all over the district. Lou, I'm almost there. I move along the railing. On my way, I watch the shadowy zones. The doors of some units are open, others have been forced. It's the eternal refrain of the disaster that's fallen on us, the traces of madness, the scratches on the wood, the black sticky fluids. I sigh. Everything was going so well up to now!

***Gus***  
> *Ambush*: A. Jorlen - Closing in fast!

In the space of a shiver, without thinking, I spin around to mow down my assailant. My armor responds at once to my will. It accompanies my motion. I feel its strength propel my arm. My baton whips the air and crashes onto the face that was about to bite. When the electric charge kicks in, the creature's expression freezes into a deformed grin. I shudder. It collapses and convulses. My blood boils in my veins; I don't want to stop there. I lift my foot up high. My boot comes down on its skull several times. I suddenly see again the face of that woman, laid out on the stairs, at my mercy. I scream. It's the only way I can stand this violence. I scream until the creature stops moving. This time, I'm not going to collapse. This time, I'm not going to cry. Stunned, I watch a pool of blood spread under my feet. Above what's left of its head, the AR label that displayed its name starts blinking. It finally fades like the mist, like the life leaving it. Once again, I couldn't help screaming like a lunatic and I feel I'm going to regret it. I already regret it: the dots on my radar are stirring; there's pounding on the apartment doors; bellows of all kinds start up again, and already three hysterics appear on the fourth-floor walkways. On my level, two more join the dance. They come out of a unit opposite Lou's. Quick! I dash toward 503. I won't stop. I ask Gus to ring the bell and as it opens, before I even set a foot inside, I find myself catapulted backward, knocked over by a beast camouflaged in the darkness of an alcove. My hip hits the railing and I nearly tip into the void. A bloated hand tries to break my helmet's visor. Between its fingers, I glimpse a man caked in blood. His suit bears the relics of his last feast.  
The other five will soon be here if I don't move my ass; I draw on my rage. I growl. With a shoulder shove, I push the attacker back, then brandish my baton like a foil. I try to hit him, but the two nearest creatures are already on me. One of them clings to my left arm. The second grabs my weapon with a full grip. The reaction is immediate. In a crackle, it twists in pain before falling backward. Its neighbor, on the other hand, tightens its grip. I get the horrible feeling it wants to crush my arm. I stagger. I catch myself on the rail. I can't defend myself in these conditions anymore, and the man in the suit comes back at me. That's all I needed. I grimace with apprehension, but a chair smashes onto his head before he has time to touch me. He drops like a sack of crap at my feet. I'm left speechless. Lou's standing behind him. Her long turquoise hair floats in the wind and I think I can make out panic-stricken fear on her face. She's defenseless and I'm stuck with the other thing dangling from my arm.

***Gus***  
> Alert: Armor - damaged 23%  
> Alert: Left sleeve - damaged 68%

I can't wait any longer. I let go of the railing to free my right hand. My assailant's weight makes me tip over with him onto the walkway floor and, before hitting the ground, I redirect the tip of my baton onto his chest. Here we are, one on top of the other. His eyes, bulging with anger, curse me. I suddenly get an idea that's going to earn me one hell of a migraine. I trigger the discharge and as the scream of the creature I'm crushing blows out my eardrum, the muscles of my arm stiffen. I don't control them anymore. Everything crackles around me. The armor-related data blinks in my field of vision, then comes back online. I very nearly fried my circuits.

***Gus***  
> Alert: Left sleeve - damaged 93%  
> Armor airtightness compromised.

No time to daydream, I get back on my feet. My left arm hurts like hell. I can barely move it. My head's spinning, but it should be okay. At my feet, the lifeless bodies of our attackers sprawl in postures of torment. I'm not sure they're dead, since their AR labels haven't disappeared, but they look like they've boiled from the inside. I look up. The three other creatures that came from the fourth are now on our floor. Lou's eyes are riveted on them. Big tears roll down her cheeks. I notice she's slightly wounded on the arm and that she's pointing the leg of her broken chair in their direction.  
"Just shoot them," she screams at me.  
I look her over without quite understanding, then I realize I've got a gun. I hand my baton to the girl. I've never fired, but we're standing in the equivalent of a corridor. Should be fine. So I draw, then I fire: nothing happens.

***Gus***  
> Are you sure you want to fire?

***Aymeric***  
> Yes for God's sake, we'll die otherwise!

***Gus***  
> Unlocking the safety.  
> Use of a class 2 weapon: police report being prepared.  
> Attempting to reach the police… In progress.  
> Unable to reach the police: local incident report issued.

What a rat, this Gus!  
"What are you doing?!" Lou screams at me, getting impatient.  
"One second, I…"  
Two apartments now separate us from the three creatures. My finger squeezes the trigger. The electric-impulse round flies out and hits the first hysteric, who falls as if he'd tripped over a rug. His body rolls, convulses, and his label vanishes. Looks like it's even more effective than my competition-grade baton. I squeeze the trigger again. I miss the second one a first time, then I finally pick him off mid-air as he leaps over his partner's corpse. His arms and legs seize up and he drops violently to the ground in a fetal position. There's one left, more on edge, more agile. He plays leapfrog, zigzagging, and methodically dodges every one of my shots. Lou suddenly takes the initiative to deal with him. I try to hold her back, but she slips away from me. I run after her. The creature stops when it sees us approaching, jumps onto the rail, and hisses before clawing the air like a transgenic tomcat. Lou doesn't give it the chance to do it again. She brandishes her baton and, without batting an eye, comes to tickle the hysteric, who ends up electrified like all the others, when he surely thought we were handing him a stick to play with. I'm left gaping at so much courage. Lou impresses me, and as the creature's body tips into the void, she throws herself forward to watch it fall. A shiver chills me when I hear the dull sound of its bones on the cobblestones of the square.  
"We've gotta split," I say, taking her weapon back from her hands.  
Lou knits her brow. She doesn't look like she agrees.  
"We save my father first!"
