# Log #6

"Brace yourself!" Fara calls to me.  
I shoot her the most questioning look, and just as I was expecting a chase down the steps to the basement, my teammate forces me over the railing. Seized by panic, I cling to the edge, but I tip over to the other side anyway and hang there like off a branch. Fara knits her brow. She's not going to jump, I think. Her head turns toward the corridor where the racket is closing in. She draws her weapon and, before I even see her use it, she shoves me backward. My fingers let go. I fall like a stone down the middle of the stairwell to land two floors below on both feet, with the elegance of a fat cat: this armor really is incredible. Despite the cushioning, a cry of pain tears out of my throat and I let myself drop to one side. My interface tells me the leg guards absorbed the impact well — I could have guessed — but Gus isn't celebrating.

***Gus***  
> Bone structures still fragile: please cease your activities.  
> Recommended rest time reassessed: six hours.

Always there to give me good news, this Gus. I groan, then close the window displaying his warnings before getting back on my feet, grimacing. Up top, Fara's gone. She's bolted. I hear a gunshot, then a second. I'm worried about her, and a bit about myself. Am I bailing like a coward? Do I wait for her? I want to run, but I don't know where to go…

***Aymeric***  
> You're not gonna sacrifice yourself for me, are you?

***Fara Nakach***  
> No. We're just changing the plan. The motorbike's off the table now.

"What!" I let slip.  
My heart pounds in my chest. I'm in no state to do the whole way on foot when we'd planned a two-up ride. Gus is going to bust my balls if I have to keep up the stunts. My teeth clench. Above me, Mike's roar echoes through the stairwell, followed by a series of heavy, fast steps. I leap out of his potential line of sight. Then I hear him moving off like a bull wrecking everything in his path. He's climbing the floors. Suddenly, I realize I've stopped breathing when a third gunshot rings out. Fara's still alive.

***Fara Nakach***  
> Good, I've got his full attention.  
> I'm going over the rooftops.

***Aymeric***  
> Hell, you don't mess around…  
> But me, what do I do?

A few seconds pass before I get an answer.

***Fara Nakach***  
> Pull yourself together.  
> Meet me behind the Saturn 8, on Commemoration Square.  
> I'll keep an eye on you.

So on top of everything, I have to do all this alone? Damn, maybe I've got a killer suit of armor, but I'm no superhero. My head spins. I feel so dumb, actually. This cop's saved my life twice and I'm still pissing myself. I sigh. I just have to grab my balls, hold on to them, and march them halfway across the district. It's like when I left my cell, except this time I've got something to defend myself with. I've got no reason to fret, it's not me that goddamn frizz-loving mutant gorilla is chasing. Okay! I pick up my baton, which had slipped from my hands, and I leave my conscience to its sissy monologue.  
I blow out a big breath.  
In front of me, if you overlook the peaceful corridor leading to the morgue, stands a double swing door under which a pool of blood has spread. From the letters I can still read on the floor, it's the parking lot. That doesn't reassure me, but I can't back out anymore; I move closer. Two portholes give me a view of what's hiding on the other side. The area looks quiet. Among the dozen or so free spots, only two are taken by cars. There's just one motorbike left. Shame the plan changed, but at least the way's clear. I close my eyes for a second. I make sure my weapon's wedged firmly in my palm, then I shake out my shoulders to pump myself up. Gus tells me the baton's battery is fully charged. Perfect! I grip it tight. It's my new favorite toy: on top of having a longer reach than the aluminum version, this one's got an electrocution system. The *Smasher* who lives in me is thrilled.  
I crack the door open, wading through the half-dried pool of blood. There's really a lot of it, so I hope whoever it belongs to is definitively dead. What I find on the other side confirms it; you don't need to be a coroner to understand what happened to him. A shiver runs down my back, even though I feel a certain detachment facing this heap of meat that's lost its humanity. Actually, I feel like I'm in front of a nature documentary, when they give us close-ups of a gazelle's carcass left abandoned, ribs in the open air. But this thing that no longer resembles anything surely had a life before all this. I sigh. Damn conscience! So I look elsewhere and step over it. The door closes behind me.

***Local Communication Lounge***

> 16 people connected within a 50m radius.  
> 7 unread messages.

The garage presents itself as a dark maw, fanged with pillars holding up the ceiling. It opens a few meters off onto the nighttime lights of the street. A dry wind rushes through it. Its rasp stirs a deep feeling of solitude in me, even though it carries the distant yelping along with it. I strain my ears to gauge the danger. Someone, or something, is pounding furiously on sheet metal, but it's not coming from here. The rest is silence over a backdrop of hysteria. The city seems changed forever. The rumble of cars and passersby is over. I feel like I've been thrown down a well in the middle of an abandoned psychiatric hospital. I shiver. There, in front of this door, I feel a bit exposed. I probably should have geared up with a riot shield. I feel vulnerable, so I lower my visor as if to shield myself from the world, I cling a little tighter to my baton to reassure myself, then I move forward, hugging the walls. When I reach the exit, I press myself against the opening and watch.  
I quickly grasp the scale of the disaster.  
The whole street is given over to blood and violence, the AR announcements keep covering the nearby facades. The more distant ones are dressed in their natural gray. They fade behind the smoke of a few fires devouring the rooftops of several buildings. Through this apocalyptic haze, I think I make out moving shadows running off into the distance. My heart pounds in my chest. My throat stings; Gus steps in. He activates the oxygen filter and forces visual assistance on me.  
In a fluorescent flash, my vision loads up with extra information. I can now make out the outlines the smoke was hiding from me, like thin lines predicting the placement of obstacles. In the middle of the street, I suddenly see the result of another massacre appear. Four corpses are piled on top of one another. They're no longer human in any way. Their legs have mutated into a kind of clawed paw, like a big cat's, and their frames, doubled in bulk, form an asymmetrical, grotesque mass of flesh. On their bellies, a gaping wound attests that their guts were ripped out. I shudder. The attacker is probably not far off. He didn't even try to feed, since the entrails unspool across the asphalt like long bloody rosaries. My stomach twists. I remember that the druids used to read the future in viscera. These announce that mine isn't very bright.  
Suddenly, a fluorescent flash bursts above me, streaking across the sky. A tooltip tells me it's Fara. My eyes go wide; I realize she's just jumped from the police station roof to land on a building across the avenue. As I see the reticle following her vanish, a second appears in pursuit. I figure it's Mike, given the size of the thing. The beast attempts the same feat in turn, but gravity reminds him that playing human cannonball can have its downsides. As his shapeless mass propels itself, I watch it end its trajectory by smashing through the bay window of one of the top floors of the very building where Fara set foot. The impact shatters the silence and wakes the few hysterics loafing around nearby.

***Local Communication Lounge***  
> 11 new connections to the lounge.  
> 27 people connected within a 50m radius.  
> 21 unread messages.

My interface goes wild as I spot a motley group of creatures cutting through the haze and running toward the building. Among them, some spill out of stopped autonomous cars and others emerge from the shadows of the surrounding alleys. Clearly, I should thank Mike for this superb diversion. After a stunt like that, I'd even offer to become his best buddy, but I don't think he'd take the request seriously. So instead I contact Fara.

***Aymeric***  
> Great jump!  
> But never ask me to do the same.  
> I'd rather die screaming.

***Fara***  
> Catching my breath.  
> Wasn't sure the armor would hold…

***Aymeric***  
> It can!  
> With all the mess he made smashing that window, Mike's got plenty to keep him busy.  
> His whole in-law family's rolling up to say hi.  
> And me, I'll be able to get out of here. Thanks!

***Fara***  
> It won't last long.  
> He's tough. He took a whole magazine.  
> Either way, do what you can to get to the Saturn 8.  
> I'll try to stay within comms range, but I'm not promising anything.

***Aymeric***  
> Sir, yes sir!

A bit of humor never hurt anyone, even if I'm not sure Fara appreciates it. I'm starting to think of myself as a real little soldier, but I'm in over my head. Still, knowing she's not far reassures me, and her performance gives me a bit of hope. With a girl like that at my side, I cut my chances of ending up as gorilla chow. I exhale. I watch, under cover. Even if the street's clear, I know nothing about all these creatures. The upside is that Commemoration Square is only a few streets from here. I pray my little stroll won't be my last. I crouch down. I hesitate: I need a bit more information.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Route  
> I want to get to the Saturn 8, without dying if possible.  
> You got that handy?

***Gus***  
> Unknown search parameters.  
> Requesting additional information from the server…  
> Internet connection severed: unable to process the request.

***Aymeric***  
> Figured as much …  
> Just show me the map of the Triangle, then.

When it comes to orientation, I'm way behind our friends the pigeons, but I manage a bit. The thing is, with all our technologies, we tend to let ourselves be guided and forget our most basic skills. Then again, I'm still floored when people ask us for help at the call center. There are sometimes idiots who get lost in a glass of water. Over time, I've learned to find my bearings.  
I narrow my eyes.  
The map appears in transparency in the middle of my field of vision. It represents our city: one of the ultra-autonomous hexagon cities the State built to manage the waves of epidemics and the massive migrations that followed the rising waters and global warming. In fifty years, these metropolises rose from the ground with the help of enormous concrete printers. I can't even imagine the organizational nightmare it must have been: digging, razing, leveling for kilometers. Humanity had never known such a worksite. They even say today that the project was more pharaonic than the seven wonders of the world combined. You don't really notice it when you live inside; it's our everyday. Then again, without a bit of AR to brighten the eyes, you'd get depressed in front of all this concrete as far as the eye can see. The designers sure didn't strain themselves on the layout. Every city in the world follows the same scheme: six triangular districts arranged in a hexagon, independent from one another and, at the center of each, a gigantic square ringed with arcades that makes up the commercial zone. There, any good citizen finds what they need for everyday life. That spot's recognizable from a distance, since a glass dome completely encloses it. And then there's the core of the hexagon, made up of the tips of the six triangles. It forms the Government Center, also called the *Town Hall* with a bit more modesty.  
With a quick glance, I analyze the possible routes.  
A countless number of streets and alleys irrigate the various subdivisions. This grid stings the eyes a bit, so I focus on the main arteries. Two peripheral roads let you move quickly from one Triangle to another. Me, I just want to reach the Saturn 8. And for that, I don't need to change districts, even if each district surely has its own Saturn 8. So I find myself facing a choice. There are two avenues that cross the triangle from tip to base. Either I head left to reach Charles de Gaulle Avenue, or I go right to reach President Trevor Avenue. I'm going to try the first option.  
I warn Fara. She tells me she'll go over the rooftops.  
Off in the distance, I hear a cry of distress followed by fresh screams. I shudder, realizing that in these concrete heaps that serve as our homes, there are still survivors at the mercy of these creatures. I hope they've found a safe place, even if the reality spread out before me isn't very cheering. In front of the police station, a score of bodies lie scattered on the ground. Among them, I make out corpses with a human appearance, but that's not all. There are also some bearing those same inconceivable shapes I came across earlier. It really looks like Mother Nature just switched on *random* mode across the whole chain of evolution.  
Time to climb out of my hole.
