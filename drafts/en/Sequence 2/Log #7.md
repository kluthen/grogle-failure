# Log #7

I stop thinking. I move along the railings of the police station and try to ignore the sounds reaching me from the building across the way. It's a party over there, ever since Mike made his crashing entrance. I leave that behind me and, just as I'm about to slip into an alley filled with smoke, my interface tells me a shape is dragging itself in my direction. I back up fast. I hide behind a trash can, gripping my baton tight. I ask Gus to replay the last few seconds for me. I freeze the image at the right moment. The creature doesn't look very sharp. It moves with a limp. So I let it pass me by. It ignores me. It heads toward the site of all the festivities, but I keep an eye on it. Its overlong arms drag like rags, and it must have swiped its jaw from a shark, since several rows of teeth jut out from between its lips. At the nape of its neck, the scalp of its skull hangs pitifully and you can guess the whiteness of its bones under a crust of blood. Yeck! For the space of a breath, I blink…  
But what I see when I open my eyes again terrifies me. In a fraction of a second, the straggler's head has just flown off. A red streak trails it onto a sidewalk where it rolls under a car. I don't understand. The body, which hasn't moved, totters and collapses to one side. Sprays of blood escape like from a fountain. You'd think you were in one of those old horror movies that didn't use virtual imagery yet. I stay gaping, I don't dare move anymore, and I replay the video. Gus surely caught something I didn't see. Frame by frame, I scroll through the scene. The result is blurry, but I manage to identify the source of the problem. Lying among the corpses piled in front of the police station, a creature was waiting patiently for something to come close. Its mandible-fitted head, mounted as if on a spring, made short work of the poor straggler. I shut off the playback. I tense up. A bead of sweat runs down my back. I'm going to have to be really careful.  
An idea suddenly comes to me: when we were kids, we used to play hide-and-seek with our AR interfaces. To help us, Gus used a very simple old protocol based on the proximity of other bracelets. I just hope I haven't uninstalled it. I rummage quickly. I cross my fingers. I find nothing. I groan. The thing is, it could be handy. We've got no more net, but Gus can give me the distance of those connected within a fifty-meter radius. For Fara, it's easier. With her permissions, she's surely got a good radar and the GPS position of every person. Me, I have to improvise.  
I try calling up the application.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus - Shall we play hide-and-seek?

***Gus***  
> Great idea, Aymeric. It's been a while.  
> I have a feeling we're going to have fun!

***Aymeric***  
> Good thing you know where I filed all this away...  
> While we're at it, switch on the *Ambush* option too.

***Gus***  
> *Ambush* option activated.  
> I'll warn you if someone approaches from behind.

Slightly off-center in my field of vision, arcs of circles appear in transparency. On the first I read the marker five meters, on the last fifty. Between them, a dozen dots are scattered, all of them more or less twenty meters from me. The interface doesn't give me the direction, but it's already something. I don't really have any idea who might come up behind me either, but Gus will alert me when someone suddenly gets within five meters. I'm already a bit reassured. I'll be able to avoid all the unwanted decapitations that stand in my path. But that's not all!

***Aymeric***  
> Gus, add some info to *Hide-and-Seek*.  
> Show Fara Nakach's dot in green.  
> And Mike Tesnov's in red, while we're at it.  
> Also add the direction of Commemoration Square.

***Gus***  
> Doing that for you right away!

The interface responds instantly. The two dots appear and a blue arrow overlays the whole thing. It reads 1370 meters. Mike's still frolicking with his new friends and Fara seems to be slowly closing in. I've no more time to waste. So I plunge fearlessly into the haze. The helmet's churning at full throttle; it picks out the trash cans, the debris, the corpses left abandoned. When the wind pushes the smoke back a little, I catch the orange glow of the fires setting the city ablaze. Baton in hand, I'm no longer afraid of anything. I come out of the alley. I turn left. The haze disappears. I'm out in the open. I crouch and move from car to trash can, from trash can to bus stop. Fara's green dot jumps five meters toward me. She's probably playing acrobat again. Mike's dot no longer shows up on the interface. I feel relieved all of a sudden. One problem solved!  
I focus on my path.  
On either side of the street, the buildings of international corporations bare their innards. Many windows are smashed. Glass litters the sidewalks and lifeless bodies cast horrified looks at me. These people took the great leap to avoid facing implacable reality. My throat knots as I step over them. The glass crunches under my steps in the silence. I hear my breathing speed up inside the armor. I feel like I'm alone here, but I spot traces of blood everywhere I lay my gaze. I try to ignore them. I try to ignore everything. I close up. I clench my teeth. The interface stays the only way to focus on something else. It gives me a reason to avoid this suffering. On my pseudo-radar, the dots stay at a good distance and I'm now at 1320 meters. I move forward. I keep moving forward, but I stop all at once; a message on the local lounge intrigues me.

***Local Communication Lounge***  
> 35 people connected within a 50m radius.  
***T. Vincent***  
> Help! I'm trapped on the top floor of Samwell Tower.  
***M. Duval***  
> Huhmone ehe emohaefhm lkfml moimefu hife… !!!  
***J. Gaousi***  
> I. The words. Forgot. Tomorrow. Make. Children. They're here!  
***T. Vincent***  
> Help! I'm trapped on the top floor of Samwell Tower.  
***D. Carmen***  
> She sells seashells by the seashore?  
***H. Lockhart***  
> Long live mice \<:3   )\~\~

Among a heap of sentences with no rhyme or reason, T. Vincent's repeats like a raft tossing between the waves, in the middle of a storm. He clearly needs help, but I hesitate. For sure Fara would have charged into the fray in super-cop mode. Bam! She'd have stormed the building with her knife between her teeth and her eyes blinking red and blue. Me, it's not confidence that's driving me tonight.

***Aymeric***  
> Hey Gus!  
> Show T. Vincent's dot in orange.

***Gus***  
> *Ambush*: T. Vincent - Closing in fast!

"What?" I shout, looking around me.  
Nobody's coming my way, yet my interface clearly tells me the orange dot is approaching at high speed. I panic. I move aside, I look up, and I understand that T. Vincent has thrown himself off the building. My breath catches and dread paralyzes me when his body smashes onto the asphalt a few meters from me. I don't know what to do anymore. My legs tremble. The impact is so brutal that the pulped flesh splatters me, but what scares me most isn't the state of the corpse. What scares me most is that, despite its suicide attempt, the thing standing before me gets back up without difficulty. I blink to rule out the possibility of a hallucination. The apparition persists. Under the glow of the streetlamps, I even make out its features: a wide face, full lips, and long blonde hair. In other circumstances I could have fallen in love, but fluids pour copiously from its skull, painting it into a terrifying mask.  
Suddenly, a new message arrives in the lounge.

***T. Vincent***  
> Help! I'm trapped on the top floor of Samwell Tower.

The harpy smiles at me. T. Vincent, that's her, and despite her enticing look, she means me no good. In one bound, she throws herself at me. I don't have time to react and I tip over backward. My armor bangs the sidewalk — Gus is going to hold it against me — and here we are face to face, the beauty and me, sprawled on the ground. Her blue eyes pierce me as she tries to pop off my shell. I try to push her away, but I can't. I'm like a big turtle flipped onto its back. Pathetic! I've still got my baton. At this distance it's hard to strike, but the electrifying side does the trick. One good jolt and the creature, wracked with spasms, pulls away screaming.  
I get back up.  
Gus throws me a new *Ambush* alert. Our little dance has drawn a crowd: two beasts, in shreds of suits, surge out of the ground floor and surround me. The beauty, meanwhile, twitches a few moments longer before coming to her senses. She's raging. I brandish my baton like a baseball bat as she charges at me once more, and in a surge of rage, I swing with everything I've got. The blow doesn't land. The blonde dodges with a leap and the two morons on the sides take advantage to jump me.  
Their claws and jaws come down on me, but this time I manage to stay steady on my feet. Gus is hollering off in his corner. I pay it no attention. Despite the blows raining down, my armor holds. I take it. I take it, but the pressure starts to rise. I feel my blood boil. If they keep up their circus, there'll soon be more of us. I grind my teeth. What's left of T. Vincent circles us. Looks like she's waiting for the right moment. I pull myself together. I exhale and, with a big crack of the pommel, I clock the guy on my right to push him back. He still wants to throw down, so with a bit of momentum, I unload another swing of the bat. This time I hit my attacker square in the head. He takes a few steps back before collapsing. It's only the beginning. I scream. I'm the hysteric now! The blonde approaches. I shove her off with my foot before trying to deliver the massive electric whack that'll really fluff up her mane.  
Damn it! She saw it coming. The moment my baton whips the air, the harpy grabs the second guy. He's the one who takes the jolt. He falls to one side and I immediately back off. I brace for the counterattack. The blonde's smiling again. She lets out a cry of fury. I have to finish this fast. She charges me. A claw swipe manages to pierce my armor. I can't let her keep going. I try to draw on my reserves. I work myself into a rage. I scream again and string together blows with the bat, my feet, my fists. The creature backs up. I push her against the steps leading inside the nearest building. She trips on them and sprawls full-length. As she tips backward, I read the surprise on her face, but I also read the hatred. For a fraction of a second, I know she's at my mercy. I go for it. My ears are ringing. I lift my foot and, with one big heel stomp, I crush her cervical vertebrae. I shudder and, as the cracking of her bones still echoes in my head, the fury that was filling me escapes. My shoulders drop.  
I collapse on the ground and sob.
