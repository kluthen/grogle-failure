# Log #27

The adrenaline drops. What it hides as it leaves is the state we're in — aches on every floor, burns the nanomachines caulked but didn't erase, a fatigue that sinks into the bones. The smell of burnt metal and charred rubber still hangs in the center. We're alive. Not presentable, but alive.

"You see," Santu says from the control room without lifting his head, both hands in the guts of a box. Here, there's enough to hold out. I've got access to electricity, the elevator's secured, we can loot the level's warehouses if it comes to that. As a fallback point, it's not bad.

Honestly, he's right. I'm not super keen on ending my days in this basement that reeks of burnt oil — but it's better than a lot of places we've slept these past few days.

I get up. In the control room, Santu's little welding sparks blink at regular intervals. The rest of the group dozes, chests rising with slow breathing. I carve a path between the bodies.

Fara's sitting against the wall right next to the elevator, visor down. She's been carrying this group from the start and no one asks if she's okay. I stop a second.

"You're not too hot in there?"

She doesn't answer. She doesn't move. Not even a crackle. The armor stays there, compact and closed, as if the question hadn't reached her. Robot-cop to the end.

I continue toward the shower. Well... it's not a simple plastic room — walk-in shower, toilet, sink, mirror. Already way classier than the one at my work. A toiletry kit in a locker, the belongings of the poor soul we neutralized in his pod. Don't care, I piss in the shower too. I let the water run a few minutes without moving. Artificial well-being at seven euros a cubic meter is worth all the gold in the world in the state I'm in.

A good soaping later, on the floor the scales of dried blood drag toward the drain. There are probably a few tears that took the same path. One of the rarely credited virtues of a good hot shower.

I look at myself in the mirror. Where the articulated welder did its grilling, the nanomachines left their mark — a silvery film over grayish skin, a slight dip, as if a millimeter or two of thickness were missing. No pain. Not a hair left on either forearm. A little snicker escapes me. Way to go, critters.

I fix my hair as best I can, scrub my teeth with a finger and toothpaste. I've got a few cracked teeth — I make a mental note to be surprised the nanomachines didn't grow me steel canines. No razor. Too bad.
