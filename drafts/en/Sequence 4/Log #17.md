# Log #17

When we pass the doors of the Hypersport, the thing that strikes you right away is the store's general state. Compared to the one we took refuge in, here it's a real carnage. Looks like a bad gore-film director blew his whole budget on expired ketchup. I plug my nose. My teammates do the same. And as we move down one of the aisles leading to the main hall, the air feels thicker than usual. It rasps my tongue. On the tiles, a sticky liquid clings to our shoes and brown smears smudge the walls. Sometimes you can make out the print of a hand on the concrete or on a store window. Except for the icy light falling from the ceiling fixtures, you'd really think you were in the final scene of *Thermo-Slicer Massacre*. Yet Olivia's face looks impassive. On the other hand, on Raoul and Cris's side, they're not acting tough anymore. As for Julian, who earlier told us he'd seen far worse during his service, he's starting to go pale. Me too, I'm not at my best: I'm nauseous.  
Even so, I try to stay as detached as possible from all this crap. I imagine we're in a B movie and that these chunks of meat surrounding us are nothing but plastic props. Well, it's still not easy, given how it reeks of old sewers. She's lucky, Fara. She's got her super helmet to protect her from these unpleasantnesses. And yet, even with her death armor, arriving in the great hall, she freezes like she's paralyzed with fear. From her video feed, I immediately understand that over there, it's even worse than in the corridor. Olivia presses against her and the rest of the group approaches in turn to take a peek. For now, me, I make do with the virtual interface; I don't like what I see in it. I clench up.  
In fact, the Hypersport is a huge building constructed over several levels filled with shelving of all kinds, fitting rooms, and play areas so any good consumer can get a feel for the gear. At the ceiling, a vast glass roof filters the afternoon light, and the red of the everlasting government messages covers the walls stripped of advertising projections. It makes the whole thing super empty and you feel literally crushed by the architecture. So, from the ground floor, the sight is all the more frightening. You see the balconies of each floor, the thick columns supporting them, and the stopped escalators serving them. Honestly, this place, when you don't have the right maps and signs, is just a goddamn maze. By the way, the teens organize — used to organize — clandestine hide-and-seek afternoons here. The security guys would chase after them. It led to some hilarious situations. But all that carefree fun is over. The space erected like a cathedral to the cult of the body has changed decorators, and this one decided to take the instructions literally by carpeting the walls, balconies, and stairs with fresh flesh. It's an unspeakable slaughterhouse. All the more since no corpse was spared: guts hang garland-style from the railings, and limbs, some of which are no longer human at all, have been scattered here and there. I can't believe my eyes. Julian stares at me with distress, then he swallows. I understand he's worried about something else. Indeed, despite the silence and the death-soaked atmosphere, I note we're far from alone here. A multitude of gray bubbles swarm. The upside in all this is that the most active of them are clustered on the upper floors or hidden far from our position.
